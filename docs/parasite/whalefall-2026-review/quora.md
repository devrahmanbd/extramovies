## Answer to paste

Yes, and the short version is that the whale is the movie and the flashbacks are what breaks it. At the time of writing it was still in US theaters and had no streaming or rental offer, so a cinema ticket is the way in, and the sound mix is the reason to spend the money on the big speakers rather than the phone.

Brian Duffield drops Austin Abrams into the stomach of a sperm whale and holds him there for most of 106 minutes. Jay Gardiner is a Californian who moved to the Midwest to get away from his father. A year after his father died and was buried in an empty casket, Jay dives alone off the edge of Monterey Bay to find him. A giant squid is fighting a sperm whale, the tentacles catch his gear, and the whale swallows both of them. He wakes up in one of the whale's stomach chambers with about an hour of air left in his tank.

The production is the achievement. The stomach models came from the Weta Workshop in New Zealand, and the head, throat, esophagus and larynx had to be built in California because they were too big to ship in time. The whale head is twelve feet tall and eighteen feet long. Empire's Simon Crook called the opening "an awesome, ominous, goosebump-plucking recreation" and described the interior lit "in sickly phosphorescent green", with the heart beating like "the artillery drum of a colossal heart counting down like a booming clock".

The acting problem is real, because Abrams's regulator is clamped in his mouth. The air inside the whale is toxic, so the film takes his voice away and leaves him wheezing, bleeding and staring. IGN's Jim Vejvoda called it "an exceptional Austin Abrams anchors the whole endeavor with grueling dedication".

What does not work is the flashbacks. Every time the film cuts to Jay as a boy on a boat with his father, the tension leaks out of a man with an hour of air left. Rotten Tomatoes' own consensus line says it: "Unforgettably visceral whenever it isn't getting mired in flashbacks." The Guardian's Benjamin Lee was blunter, saying it "just doesn't work as a 106-minute adventure, too overstretched and too silly to have us rooting for his escape", and The Austin Chronicle called it a tonal mess.

On the numbers, Rotten Tomatoes has it at 72 percent from 168 critic reviews and Metacritic has it at 59 from 37, with a Letterboxd member average of 3.25 out of 5 from 92,965 ratings and a CinemaScore of B. That split is the film. It cost $60 million and opened to roughly $8 million in the United States on 3,065 screens.

PG-13, 106 minutes, directed by Brian Duffield, adapted from Daniel Kraus's 2023 novel.
