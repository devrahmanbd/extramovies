Whalefall (2026)
Dir. Brian Duffield | 106 min | PG-13 | US release October 16, 2026

Yes, and see it in a cinema with the sound turned up. Brian Duffield drops Austin Abrams into the stomach of a sperm whale and holds him there for 106 minutes, and for long stretches the film is so convincingly built that the whale stops being a trick. Then the flashbacks start pulling, and a survival thriller turns into a son having it out with his dead father. Rotten Tomatoes has it at 72 percent from 168 critic reviews and Metacritic has it at 59 from 37. Both numbers are right.

The premise is as plain as it gets. Jay Gardiner is a Californian who decamped to the Midwest to get away from his father. Mitt Gardiner was the local dive-boat captain, and Entertainment Weekly opens the film on the end of his life: mesothelioma, sealed letters for his wife and three children left in a cooler on the boat, then dive weights and a jump into the ocean. The body is never found, and the family buries an empty casket. A year later Jay dives alone to find it, off the edge of Monterey Bay, without telling his mother or his sisters. A giant squid is fighting a sperm whale. The squid's tentacles catch his gear and the whale swallows both of them. Jay wakes up in one of the whale's stomach chambers with about an hour of air left in his tank, and the film is about what he does with it.

The set is the achievement. Simon Crook at Empire called the opening, a giant squid fighting a sperm whale with Jay caught in the middle, "an awesome, ominous, goosebump-plucking recreation", and described the interior lit "in sickly phosphorescent green" with a heart working like "the artillery drum of a colossal heart counting down like a booming clock". The production built the thing rather than implying it. The stomach models came from the Weta Workshop in New Zealand, while the head, throat, esophagus and larynx had to be built in California because they were too big to ship in time. The whale head is twelve feet tall and eighteen feet long.

The camera and the sound do the rest. Aaron Morton shot it, and Zachary Lee at RogerEbert.com noticed how he frames Jay "as though his surroundings are assaulting him". On the sound, Lee was brief: the sequences are amplified by a sound department that "makes it all feel otherworldly". The whale's interior is a place you hear before you understand.

Abrams does the heavy lifting mostly without words. His regulator is clamped in his mouth because the air inside the whale is toxic, so the film takes his voice away and leaves him wheezing, bleeding and staring. IGN's Jim Vejvoda called him "an exceptional Austin Abrams anchors the whole endeavor with grueling dedication".

The failure is the flashbacks. Rotten Tomatoes' own consensus catches the fault line: "Unforgettably visceral whenever it isn't getting mired in flashbacks." Every time the film cuts away to Jay as a boy on a boat with his father, the tension leaks, because the film trades a man with an hour of air for a man being told off. The third act is worse. Benjamin Lee in The Guardian was blunt: "it just doesn't work as a 106-minute adventure, too overstretched and too silly to have us rooting for his escape". He thought the picture could have been a lean 85-minute race to survive. Richard Whittaker in The Austin Chronicle put the diagnosis in one line: "Whalefall is a tonal mess". A movie that puts gross-out comedy next to a family tearjerker and expects you to enjoy both.

Brolin plays Mitt as a hard man with a hard mouth, and The Daily Beast's Nick Schager thought the performance overshoots, asking him to play "such a grade-A jerk that he invariably chews the scenery". Elisabeth Shue plays Jay's mother and gets the least of the movie, and Jane Levy and Emily Rudd play the sisters and are given almost nothing to do.

On the numbers. Rotten Tomatoes has it at 72 percent from 168 critic reviews, with the top critics at 64 percent from 33, and a Popcornmeter of 81 percent from more than 500 verified ratings. Metacritic has a Metascore of 59 from 37 critic reviews, labelled Mixed or Average, split 54 percent positive, 41 percent mixed and 5 percent negative, with a user score of 6.7 from 28 ratings. Letterboxd members have it at a weighted 3.25 out of 5 from 92,965 ratings and 52,550 written reviews. CinemaScore came back with a B. The split is the story: on Metacritic the scores run from a 90 at Collider to a 30 at The Austin Chronicle, and the people who loved the film and the people who did not were watching the same 106 minutes.

At the time of writing it was in theaters with no streaming offer for it in the US. It cost $60 million and opened to roughly $8 million in the United States on 3,065 screens, finishing fourth in a weekend led by Street Fighter.

The whale is the movie. The father is the theme the movie keeps interrupting itself to make. Watch it with the volume up, because the last thirty minutes are where the picture decides what it thinks it is.
