Hope (2026)
Dir. Na Hong-jin | 157 min | R | South Korea, original title 호프 (Hopeu) | US release September 9, 2026

Worth watching for its first hour, which is one of the most exhilarating stretches of action in recent Korean cinema. The rest is a different proposition. Na Hong-jin's creature epic, his first feature since The Wailing, turned a village near the DMZ into a war zone and then kept turning. The problems arrive after the hour mark. The creature's CGI stutters, the characters stop existing, and the last ten minutes hand out lore without resolution.

First, the disambiguation, because the title is useless on its own. Three films have been called Hope. The 2021 Norwegian drama and the 2013 Korean film are not this one. This is the 2026 South Korean science fiction horror from the director of The Chaser and The Wailing. It played in South Korean cinemas from July 15, 2026, ran the festival circuit through New York, Melbourne, Toronto and Zurich, premiered in competition at Cannes in May, and opened in the United States on September 9, 2026.

The village is Hope Harbor, close enough to the demilitarised zone to be lined with signs telling residents not to trust strangers, and most of the people living in it are old. Police chief Ko Bum-seok, played by Hwang Jung-min, is called out to a dead cow with gaping wounds lying in the middle of the road. His cousin Ko Sung-ki, played by Zo In-sung, and a hunting party take the forest to track whatever did it.

Bum-seok gets back to town to find the place already cut open, buildings with holes punched through them and bodies in the alleyways, and something running through it that kills people and cars in the same breath. The creature itself stays out of sight for almost an hour, and Bum-seok spends that time chasing it on foot and in a patrol car while officer Im Sung-ae, played by Hoyeon, keeps up. It is finally shown, then hit by a grenade launcher, and then run down by a tanker, and the film lets you think the business is finished. Meanwhile the hunting party goes deeper into the mountains, which is where the answers and the worst of it both are.

What works is the first hour. Robert Daniels at RogerEbert.com filed his review from the Cannes premiere and wrote that "the first hour of 'Hope' rips". The chase through the wrecked village is built on long tracking shots that stay level with Bum-seok as he runs, and Daniels said they are "so smooth it's difficult to figure out how Na and his cinematographer Hong Kyung-pyo pulled them off". Empire's Harry Stainer put the same feeling in a different key: "Not since Mad Max: Fury Road has a film slammed its foot down on the accelerator so hard."

Hoyeon is the find of the film. Sung-ae is a police officer who also happens to be trained for field hospitals, and she spends the opening hour firing a grenade launcher and handling a rifle like someone who has used both. Stainer's read is the best one written: "it is Hoyeon, who enters the film firing off a grenade-launcher, who cements herself as an action star here, balancing the film's more tender moments while being equally convincing firing off thousands of rifle rounds."

The chaos is its own reward. The film keeps switching registers mid-sentence, from a car chase to a joke to something unbearable, and it never explains the joke. Stainer described it as "an insane two-and-a-half-hour monster movie with a brutal 'how-the-hell-did-they-film-that?' foot-to-horse-to-car extended chase, a serious lengthy monologue about diarrhoea (which is a crucial plot point), and, to top it all off, Michael Fassbender and Alicia Vikander rock up at one point".

What does not work is the creature. Robert Abele in the Los Angeles Times wrote that "the rickety CGI not its strong suit, 'Hope' never quite follows through on its crafty first hour of peekaboo velocity". David Ehrlich at IndieWire went further and called them "some of the worst creature effects this side of the Syfy Channel or 'The Mummy Returns'". The second half loses the plot Daniels was praising: the last two-thirds of the film "seemingly lose all momentum", and the characters thin out, with the camera pulling back and shooting the hero like an anybody. The Atlantic's David Sims felt the size of the thing the whole way through and said that "Everything could perhaps be a little bit smaller". Stainer saw a structural problem too, writing that "A lore-heavy final ten minutes makes clear this is only Part 1".

On the numbers. Rotten Tomatoes has the Tomatometer at 79 percent from 177 reviews and the Popcornmeter at 77 percent from more than 500 verified ratings. Metacritic has a Metascore of 67 from 37 critic reviews, with a user score of 6.1 from 77 ratings. IMDb has it at 6.7 from 21,000 votes. Letterboxd members have it at a member average of 3.19 from 180,756 ratings and 98,847 written reviews.

The scale was the gamble. IndieWire's David Ehrlich reported that Hope is the most expensive film ever made in Korea, with a budget of about 50 billion won, and Empire wrote that it "has made headlines as South Korea's most expensive movie of all time".

At the time of writing there was no subscription streaming for it in the United States. It was listed for digital purchase or rental on Amazon Video, the Apple TV Store and Fandango At Home, plus a rental on Plex, with the digital release on October 13, 2026. Watch the listing carefully on any streaming site, because there are two other films called Hope.

Take the first hour. It is worth the ticket on its own.
