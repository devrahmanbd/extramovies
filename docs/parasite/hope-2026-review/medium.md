## Title

Is Hope (2026) worth watching? The first hour says yes, the rest says less

## Subtitle

Na Hong-jin's Korean monster epic hides the creature for sixty minutes and then forgets it for the other ninety-seven.

---

## Copy to paste

Hope (2026) is worth watching for its first hour, which is one of the most exhilarating stretches of action in recent Korean cinema. The rest is a different proposition. Na Hong-jin's creature epic, his first feature since The Wailing, turned a village near the DMZ into a war zone and then kept turning. The problems arrive after the hour mark: the creature's effects stutter, the characters stop existing, and the last ten minutes hand out lore without resolution.

First, the disambiguation, because the title on its own is useless. Three films have been called Hope. The 2021 Norwegian drama and the 2013 Korean film are not this one. This is the 2026 South Korean science fiction horror from the director of The Chaser and The Wailing, original title 호프 (Hopeu). It played in South Korean cinemas from July 15, 2026, ran the festival circuit through New York, Melbourne, Toronto and Zurich, premiered in competition at Cannes in May, and opened in the United States on September 9, 2026.

## The village and the hour of hiding

The village is Hope Harbor, close enough to the demilitarised zone to be lined with signs telling residents not to trust strangers, and most of the people living in it are old. Police chief Ko Bum-seok, played by Hwang Jung-min, is called out to a dead cow with gaping wounds lying in the middle of the road. His cousin Ko Sung-ki, played by Zo In-sung, and a hunting party take the forest to track whatever did it.

## The first hour rips

Robert Daniels at RogerEbert.com filed his review from the Cannes premiere and wrote that "the first hour of 'Hope' rips". The chase through the wrecked village is built on long tracking shots that stay level with Bum-seok as he runs, and Daniels said they are "so smooth it's difficult to figure out how Na and his cinematographer Hong Kyung-pyo pulled them off". Empire's Harry Stainer put the same feeling in a different key: "Not since Mad Max: Fury Road has a film slammed its foot down on the accelerator so hard."

Hoyeon is the find of the film. Sung-ae is a police officer who also happens to be trained for field hospitals, and she spends the opening hour firing a grenade launcher and handling a rifle like someone who has used both. Stainer's read is the best one written: "it is Hoyeon, who enters the film firing off a grenade-launcher, who cements herself as an action star here, balancing the film's more tender moments while being equally convincing firing off thousands of rifle rounds."

## Then the monster shows up and the film shrinks

Robert Abele in the Los Angeles Times wrote that "the rickety CGI not its strong suit, 'Hope' never quite follows through on its crafty first hour of peekaboo velocity". David Ehrlich at IndieWire went further and called them "some of the worst creature effects this side of the Syfy Channel or 'The Mummy Returns'".

The second half is a structural problem as much as an effects one. Daniels said the last two-thirds of the film "seemingly lose all momentum", and that "Every character here is seemingly underwritten. We barely find out anything about any of them." He also noticed the camera changing its mind about who the hero is, shooting Bum-seok in hero shots for the first hour and then pulling back and shooting him like an anybody for the rest of the film. The Atlantic's David Sims felt the size of the thing the whole way through and said that "Everything could perhaps be a little bit smaller". Stainer saw it as a story problem: "A lore-heavy final ten minutes makes clear this is only Part 1, and may leave some viewers questioning what on earth they just saw."

## The numbers

Rotten Tomatoes has the Tomatometer at 79 percent from 177 reviews and the Popcornmeter at 77 percent from more than 500 verified ratings. Metacritic has a Metascore of 67 from 37 critic reviews, split 65 percent positive, 30 percent mixed and 5 percent negative, plus a user score of 6.1 from 77 ratings. IMDb has it at 6.7 from 21,000 votes. Letterboxd members have it at 3.19 from 180,756 ratings and 98,847 written reviews. The platform's critics consensus calls it "an unhinged sci-fi epic that boasts awe-inspiring production design and amusingly quaint visual effects", which is a fair description of both halves.

## Where to watch it

At the time of writing there was no subscription streaming for it in the United States. It was listed for digital purchase or rental on Amazon Video, the Apple TV Store and Fandango At Home, plus a rental on Plex, with the digital release on October 13, 2026. Check the listing carefully on any streaming site, because there are two other films called Hope.

## The short version

Take the first hour. It is worth the ticket on its own. Na Hong-jin has made the most entertaining Korean monster film in years and then let the monster disappear from his own movie, because the second hour has nothing to replace it.
