## The question this answers

Is Hope (2026) worth watching? Which Hope is this, and where can I watch it in the US?

## Answer to paste

Worth watching for its first hour, which is one of the most exhilarating stretches of action in recent Korean cinema. The rest is a different proposition.

Start with the disambiguation, because the title on its own is useless and there are two films it is not. The 2021 Norwegian drama and the 2013 Korean film are not this one. This is the 2026 South Korean science fiction horror by Na Hong-jin, the director of The Chaser and The Wailing, original title 호프 (Hopeu). It premiered in competition at Cannes in May 2026, played in South Korean cinemas from July 15, ran the festival circuit through New York, Melbourne, Toronto and Zurich, and opened in the United States on September 9, 2026 through Neon.

The village is Hope Harbor, close enough to the demilitarised zone to be lined with signs telling residents not to trust strangers, and most of the people living in it are old. Police chief Ko Bum-seok, played by Hwang Jung-min, is called out to a dead cow with gaping wounds in the middle of the road. A hunting party led by his cousin, played by Zo In-sung, goes into the forest to find what did it.

The creature stays off screen for almost an hour, and that restraint is the film's best idea. Bum-seok spends it chasing on foot and in a patrol car while officer Im Sung-ae, played by Hoyeon, keeps up. When the creature is finally shown, it is hit by a grenade launcher and then run down by a tanker, and the film lets you think the business is finished.

The first hour is the reason to go. RogerEbert.com's Robert Daniels filed from the Cannes premiere and wrote that "the first hour of 'Hope' rips", and that the long tracking shots through the wrecked village are "so smooth it's difficult to figure out how Na and his cinematographer Hong Kyung-pyo pulled them off". Empire's Harry Stainer wrote that "Not since Mad Max: Fury Road has a film slammed its foot down on the accelerator so hard", and Hoyeon cements herself as an action star in the process.

What does not survive the hour mark is the creature. The Los Angeles Times wrote that "the rickety CGI not its strong suit, 'Hope' never quite follows through on its crafty first hour of peekaboo velocity", and the second half loses momentum, with the characters going thin and a lore-heavy final ten minutes that makes clear this is only Part 1.

On the numbers, Rotten Tomatoes has it at 79 percent from 177 critic reviews with a Popcornmeter at 77 percent, Metacritic has it at 67 from 37 critic reviews with a user score of 6.1 from 77 ratings, IMDb has it at 6.7 from 21,000 votes, and Letterboxd members have it at 3.19 from 180,756 ratings. It was the most expensive film ever made in Korea, with a budget of about 50 billion won.

On availability: at the time of writing there was no US subscription service. It was listed for digital purchase or rental on Amazon Video, the Apple TV Store and Fandango At Home, plus a rental on Plex, with the digital release on October 13, 2026. Check the title carefully on any streaming site.

R, 157 minutes, directed by Na Hong-jin.
