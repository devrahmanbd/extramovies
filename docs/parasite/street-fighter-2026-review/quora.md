## The question this answers

Is the new Street Fighter movie (2026) worth watching? And where can I watch it in the US?

## Answer to paste

Worth watching for the fights, and for not much else. If you grew up on the arcade cabinet, the fight work will feel right in a way few game adaptations manage. If you need the story to hold together, this one does not.

Here is what you get. The film opens in 1987, in a fully animated anime prologue, at the first World Warrior tournament. Ryu beats Sagat with the Dark Hadou, the demon Akuma notices, and Ryu is left with two problems: his master Gouken, whose death comes soon after the fight, and an adoptive brother, Ken Masters, who blames him for the rift. Ryu disappears into a life of handing out ramen on the street. Ken becomes a celebrity doing commercials for colas and batteries.

Six years later, in 1993, M. Bison stages the second World Warrior tournament in Metrolao City, and Chun-Li, an undercover Interpol agent working on her father's murder, recruits both men. Bison's real plan is a device that pulls the fighting power out of a champion. By the middle of the picture, Ryu's own story has been pushed aside for the machine and the family argument.

The fights are shot through artificial grain and 1980s neon, and the choreography uses the game's actual special moves with the vocal callouts, so a Hadouken reads as a Hadouken. The fully animated sequences are the best-looking, best-sounding moments in the picture. The A.V. Club's Jacob Oller called them "by far the best-looking, best-sounding, most coherent moments of the movie", and Helen O'Hara at Empire wrote that "the sucker-punch is that it's actually quite fun".

The problem is the script, which has four fighters and not enough room. Oller called the result a film that "merely pratfalls for two excruciating hours of bad jokes, worse combat, and a plot that crumbles under the weight of its character select screen". RogerEbert.com's Clint Worthington put it another way, saying the punishing two-hour runtime ends in a flagrant call for a sequel.

On the numbers, this is the cleanest split in this batch. Rotten Tomatoes has it at 42 percent from 120 critic reviews, with a Popcornmeter at 79 percent from more than 1,000 verified ratings. Metacritic has a user score of 6.8 from 174 ratings, and Letterboxd members have it at 2.81 from 103,183 ratings and 55,024 written reviews. Critics disliked it. Most of the people who bought a ticket did not.

On availability: at the time of writing it was still in US cinemas with no subscription service for it in the United States. The way in was a purchase, with the digital release on Fandango At Home, and the disc from Amazon DVD / Blu-ray. It opened on October 16, 2026.

PG-13, 120 minutes, directed by Kitao Sakurai.
