## Title

Avengers: Endgame Encore: is the 2026 cinema re-release worth it?

## Subtitle

The 2019 finale is back on the big screen with about four extra minutes and a sequel bridge. The verdict belongs to the original cut.

---

## Copy to paste

Yes, Avengers: Endgame is worth watching, and if you missed it in 2019 the 2026 Encore edition is the cheapest and best excuse to see it on a cinema screen. If you did see it in 2019, the question is whether a room full of strangers is worth the ticket. Marvel is running the film again from September 25, 2026, with new material attached, roughly four extra minutes of running time and the first release to carry the new Infinity Vision certification, ahead of Avengers: Doomsday in December.

The important thing to say first is that the film underneath has not been retooled. The extra minutes are a bridge to a sequel, not a revision of the 2019 story. So the criticism of the film itself still applies exactly as it did seven years ago, and anyone spending three hours in a cinema should be judging the 181-minute cut the critics saw.

## Why the performances still hold it up

The cast is the reason the film still lands. Brian Tallerico wrote at RogerEbert.com that Chris Evans and Robert Downey Jr. "have turned Captain America and Iron Man into something larger than life for a generation." Evans plays Steve Rogers as a tired man holding a job he no longer believes in, and the film is smart enough to make the tiredness the point rather than the weakness.

## The hour before the suits

What works is the first hour, when nobody is in a suit. The opening moves from a compound reunion into a funeral, and it lets characters sit in a room with each other before it needs them. Tallerico credited the screenplay with the change in the film's posture: "one feels, for arguably the first time, a sense of looking back instead of merely trying to set the table for something to come." The colour-coded time travel suits are a cheap joke that lands because the grief underneath them is played straight. David Sims at The Atlantic made the same point from the other side, noting that the film "earns its length not by overstuffing the frame with opulent action, but by slowing things down and basking in the charisma of its ensemble."

## Where the craft shows its seams

Tallerico's complaint is about craft rather than taste: "The result is a film that often feels more like a product than a piece of art." He wanted the pauses about the seriousness of the situation to lead somewhere that felt spontaneous. The middle hour delivers a lot of spectacle that is very precisely calibrated and never quite wobbles.

There is also the inheritance problem. Returning characters get their heroism back rather than earning it in the moment, and three hours is long enough for that to show. Bradshaw put it as: "Sceptics might feel assailed by the 181-minute running time; a three-hour movie is the ultimate act of fan service." The runtime is not the problem on its own. The problem is that the second half is a series of set pieces joined by scenes of people saying goodbye.

## The numbers, which are the argument

Rotten Tomatoes has the film at 94 percent from 553 critic reviews, with a 96 percent Popcornmeter from more than 2,500 verified ratings. Metacritic has a Metascore of 78 from 57 critic reviews, labelled Generally Favorable, plus a user score of 8.3 from 5,786 user ratings. IMDb has it at 8.4 out of 10 from 1.5 million ratings. Letterboxd members have it at a weighted 4.03 from 4,180,355 ratings, with 495,274 written reviews.

The gap between 94 and 78 is the whole argument. The 553 critics on Rotten Tomatoes and the audiences treated the finale as a shared event. The 57 critics on Metacritic treated it as a machine with a moving part where a soul should be.

The encore ran 47 markets between September 23 and September 25, 2026 and took $141,892,440, which took the film's lifetime worldwide total to $2,941,331,540 and made it the highest-grossing film of all time again. The original cut ran in 57 markets from April 24 to April 29, 2019 and took $2,799,439,100.

In the US it is on Disney Plus, with rental and purchase on Amazon Video and the Apple TV Store.

## The short version

Go to a cinema for the encore if you can, because the film was built for a room with strangers in it, and the encore gives you a reason to go on release night. The 2019 cut is a near-perfect goodbye held together by craft rather than by human mess, and anyone who has outgrown the franchise will find the seams faster than the fans will.
