Avengers: Endgame (2019)
Dir. Anthony Russo, Joe Russo | 181 min | PG-13 | Encore edition in cinemas from September 25, 2026

Yes, this is worth watching, and the 2026 encore edition is the best excuse in seven years to put it back on a cinema screen. The verdict belongs to the 2019 film by Anthony and Joe Russo. Marvel is running it again as Avengers Endgame Encore from September 25, 2026, with new material attached and roughly four extra minutes of running time. The cut underneath has not been retooled, so the criticism of the film itself still applies exactly as it did in 2019.

The performances are the reason the film still lands. Brian Tallerico wrote at RogerEbert.com that Chris Evans and Robert Downey Jr. "have turned Captain America and Iron Man into something larger than life for a generation." Evans plays Steve Rogers as a tired man holding a job he no longer believes in, and the film is smart enough to make the tiredness the point rather than the weakness. Jeremy Renner gets the rawest material in the picture, with Clint Barton opening the movie on the night the snap takes his family. Chris Hemsworth plays Thor as a bereaved man who has eaten himself into a shape he cannot get out of, and makes the grief funny without letting you off the hook for it. Mark Ruffalo's Bruce Banner is a professor giving a TED talk on the day the world ends, and Paul Rudd plays Scott Lang with a loose energy that keeps the science from turning into homework.

What works is the first hour, when nobody is in a suit. The opening moves from a compound reunion into a funeral, and it lets characters sit in a room with each other before it needs them. Tallerico credited the screenplay with the change in the film's posture: "one feels, for arguably the first time, a sense of looking back instead of merely trying to set the table for something to come." David Sims at The Atlantic made the same point from the other side, noting that the film "earns its length not by overstuffing the frame with opulent action, but by slowing things down and basking in the charisma of its ensemble."

What does not work is the engineering. Tallerico's complaint is about craft rather than taste: "The result is a film that often feels more like a product than a piece of art." The middle hour delivers a lot of spectacle that is very precisely calibrated and never quite wobbles. The other problem is that returning characters inherit their heroism back rather than earning it in the moment. Peter Bradshaw at The Guardian put it as: "Sceptics might feel assailed by the 181-minute running time; a three-hour movie is the ultimate act of fan service." The runtime is not the problem on its own. The problem is that the second half is a series of set pieces joined by scenes of people saying goodbye.

On the numbers, and all of these are the 2019 film's record, because the encore is the same film: Rotten Tomatoes has it at 94 percent from 553 critic reviews, with a 96 percent Popcornmeter from more than 2,500 verified ratings. Metacritic has a Metascore of 78 from 57 critic reviews, labelled Generally Favorable, plus a user score of 8.3 from 5,786 user ratings. IMDb has it at 8.4 out of 10 from 1.5 million ratings. Letterboxd members have it at a weighted 4.03 from 4,180,355 ratings, with 495,274 written reviews.

The gap between the 94 and the 78 is the whole argument. The 553 critics on Rotten Tomatoes and the audiences treated the finale as a shared event. The 57 critics on Metacritic treated it as a machine with a moving part where a soul should be.

For the encore specifically: it ran 47 markets between September 23 and September 25, 2026, took $141,892,440, and pushed the film's lifetime worldwide total to $2,941,331,540, which made it the highest-grossing film of all time again. The original 181-minute cut ran in 57 markets from April 24 to April 29, 2019 and took $2,799,439,100. The extra minutes are a bridge to a sequel, not a revision of the 2019 story, so anyone spending three hours in a cinema should be judging the cut the critics saw.

In the US it is on Disney Plus, with rental and purchase on Amazon Video and the Apple TV Store.

Go to a cinema for the encore if you can, because the film was built for a room with strangers in it. The 2019 cut is a near-perfect goodbye that is held together by craft rather than by human mess, and anyone who has outgrown the franchise will find the seams faster than the fans will.
