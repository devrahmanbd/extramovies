## The question this answers

Is Avengers: Endgame worth watching again in 2026? Is the Encore edition different from the original 2019 movie, and is it worth going to a cinema for it?

## Answer to paste

Yes, and the encore edition is the best excuse in seven years to put it back on a big screen. Marvel is running the film again from September 25, 2026 with new material attached, roughly four extra minutes of running time, and the first release carrying the new Infinity Vision certification ahead of Avengers: Doomsday in December.

Here is the part worth being precise about. The cut underneath has not been retooled. The extra minutes are a bridge to a sequel, not a revision of the 2019 story. So if you are deciding whether to spend three hours in a cinema, judge the 181-minute cut the critics saw. The encore is a reason to go, not a different film.

The film itself is a near-perfect goodbye held together by craft rather than by human mess. The performances are the reason it still lands. Chris Evans plays Steve Rogers as a tired man holding a job he no longer believes in, and the film is smart enough to make the tiredness the point rather than the weakness. Robert Downey Jr. carries the other end of it. RogerEbert.com's Brian Tallerico wrote that the two of them "have turned Captain America and Iron Man into something larger than life for a generation."

What works is the first hour, when nobody is in a suit. The opening moves from a compound reunion into a funeral and lets the characters sit in a room with each other before it needs them. David Sims at The Atlantic said the film "earns its length not by overstuffing the frame with opulent action, but by slowing things down and basking in the charisma of its ensemble." The Guardian's Peter Bradshaw found the same priority, writing that the catharsis in the final battle "arises from prioritising character development over plot and spectacle."

The criticism is about craft and about length. Tallerico called the result a film that "often feels more like a product than a piece of art," and Bradshaw noted that "a three-hour movie is the ultimate act of fan service." The runtime is not the problem on its own. The second half is a series of set pieces joined by scenes of people saying goodbye.

On the numbers, Rotten Tomatoes has the 2019 film at 94 percent from 553 critic reviews, with a 96 percent Popcornmeter from more than 2,500 verified ratings. Metacritic has it at 78 from 57 critic reviews, labelled Generally Favorable, plus a user score of 8.3 from 5,786 user ratings. IMDb has it at 8.4 out of 10 from 1.5 million ratings. Letterboxd members have it at a weighted 4.03 from 4,180,355 ratings. That gap between 94 and 78 is the argument in miniature: a large panel treated the finale as a shared event, a small panel treated it as a machine.

Box office, for context on the encore: the 2019 run took $2,799,439,100 worldwide, the 2026 encore took $141,892,440 across 47 markets, and the lifetime total of $2,941,331,540 put it back at the top of the all-time chart.

In the US it is on Disney Plus, with rental and purchase on Amazon Video and the Apple TV Store.
