## Title

Resident Evil review: the 2026 reboot got the horror right

## Subtitle

Zach Cregger built the first film in this franchise on monsters, jokes and a courier who cannot shoot. Only the last act gets away from him.

---

## Copy to paste

Yes, Resident Evil is worth watching, and it is worth the ninety-five minutes it asks for. Zach Cregger's reboot is the first film in this franchise built on horror instead of noise. Austin Abrams carries it with a performance that is funny, frightened and completely physical. The last act gets away from the director. Everything before that is a real thrill ride.

Here is the part that surprises people who wrote this franchise off. The set-up is tiny and it does all the work. Bryan Hodukavich is a medical courier and his shift is over. His girlfriend has just told him she is pregnant. He takes one more run anyway, up a mountain road in a snowstorm, in a car with no chains on the tires, carrying a Schedule A package he assumes is a human heart. He hits a woman who steps into the snow, finds her lying in the woods, puts her in the passenger seat, and gets pulled over by a deputy sheriff who offers to help. Then the night starts.

Twenty minutes in, Bryan is on foot in a blizzard holding a shotgun he has never fired, and the film has laid out its grammar: a run of self-contained missions, each one a location to cross and a problem you get one attempt at.

## The games are in the structure, not the lore

Brian Tallerico at RogerEbert.com spotted the props that give it away. There is an old typewriter in an upstairs room, and there is a green plant in an abandoned car. Those are a save point and a health pickup. Jesse Hassenger at The Guardian caught the premise exactly, a man dropped into a video game before learning the proper controls, and picked the detail that sells the whole thing: by the time his life has been endangered several times over, Bryan is complaining about his wet socks.

That is the difference between this and the other seven films in the series. The lore sits at arm's length. A pharmaceutical company, a laboratory, an outbreak caused by a serum designed to make organisms evolve faster. The game's ideas are in the grammar of the film, not in its plot.

## The creature, and the comedy under it

The image the film sells itself with is a dog that pushes its head through a barn wall and then splits open like a flower. It is wet, physical, and built to be looked at. Tallerico pointed out that Cregger's approach is not straight jump scares but "unpredictable surprises built around creatures that aren't quite human", and compared the effect to riding in a truck with Large Marge in Pee-Wee's Big Adventure. On the photography, he noted that the creatures keep coming in and out of the light and that the cinematographer, Wolski, finds an aesthetic that is not overly oppressive.

Then there is the tone, which is the actual surprise. William Bibbiani at TheWrap put it best: "It's a great movie. It's an explosive horror experience which, unlike every other 'Resident Evil' adaptation, captures the strange dichotomy of this popular video game series. It starts out terrifying, gradually becomes ridiculous, and totally gets away with that tonal shift." The picture is funny the way the games are funny, in the middle of the danger, and it never apologises for the switch.

## The last act wobbles

The criticism everyone agrees on is the tail. Tallerico wrote that "The final scenes also feel like they get away from Cregger a bit, clunkier than the rest of the movie. He seems to have way more fun setting up the chaos than being forced to conclude it", a complaint some levelled at his first two films as well. Hassenger heard it too and judged it more gently, calling it "more a sin of omission than a big misstep". There is also a thread that never pays off: the pregnancy hanging over Bryan is asked for and never tested.

## The numbers

Rotten Tomatoes has the film at 96 percent from 300 critic reviews, with a 90 percent Popcornmeter from more than 10,000 verified ratings. Metacritic has a Metascore of 79 from 51 critic reviews, labelled Generally Favorable, plus a user score of 7.4 from 804 user ratings. IMDb has it at 7.6 out of 10 from 111,000 ratings. Letterboxd members have it at a weighted 3.75 from 1,599,062 ratings, with 776,456 written reviews.

At the time of writing there was no streaming offer for it in the US. It was still in theaters, and the only thing available to buy online was a Blu-ray.

## The short version

Watch it as a horror movie with a comedy running under it, not as a franchise instalment that owes you continuity. Cregger took a name with a bad history and built something with craft under it: a courier who cannot shoot, a farmhouse dog that splits open, a single night that keeps escalating.
