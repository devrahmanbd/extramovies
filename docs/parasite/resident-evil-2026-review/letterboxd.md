Resident Evil (2026)
Dir. Zach Cregger | 95 min | R | US release September 18, 2026

Yes, and it is worth the ninety-five minutes it asks for. This is the first film in the franchise built on horror instead of noise. Austin Abrams carries it with a performance that is funny, frightened and completely physical. The last act gets away from the director. Everything before that is a real thrill ride.

The set-up is small and it works. Bryan Hodukavich is a medical courier and his shift is over. His girlfriend has just told him she is pregnant. He takes one more run anyway, up a mountain road in a snowstorm, in a car with no chains on the tires, carrying a Schedule A package he assumes is a human heart. He hits a woman who steps into the snow, finds her lying in the woods, puts her in the passenger seat, and gets pulled over by a deputy sheriff who offers to help. Then the night starts. Twenty minutes in, Bryan is on foot in a blizzard holding a shotgun he has never fired, and the film has laid out its grammar: a run of self-contained missions, each one a location to cross and a problem you get one attempt at.

The games live in the structure rather than the lore. Brian Tallerico at RogerEbert.com spotted the props, an old typewriter in an upstairs room and a green plant in an abandoned car, which are save points and a health pickup. Jesse Hassenger at The Guardian caught the premise exactly, a man dropped into a video game before learning the proper controls, and noted the detail that sells it: by the time his life has been endangered several times over, Bryan is complaining about his wet socks.

The creature design is the image the film sells itself with. The dog that pushes its head through a barn wall and then splits open like a flower is wet, physical, and built to be looked at. Tallerico pointed out that Cregger's approach is not straight jump scares but "unpredictable surprises built around creatures that aren't quite human", and compared the effect to riding in a truck with Large Marge in Pee-Wee's Big Adventure.

Abrams is the reason the film works. Tallerico called it "a fantastic piece of work, the kind of physical acting that often doesn't get enough credit", and said Abrams does not turn Bryan into an action hero but an ordinary guy trying to do something important. Hassenger put it in terms of charm, describing Abrams as carrying "a Campbell-ish charm, even in the face of his character's repeated blundering, as well as an everyman relatability". Around him, Zach Cherry plays Carl, Paul Walter Hauser plays Paul and Will Merrick plays Dave, the three Umbrella scientists waiting on the package, with Kali Reis as Maxine, an Umbrella enforcer.

The tone is the surprise. William Bibbiani at TheWrap put it well: "It's a great movie. It's an explosive horror experience which, unlike every other 'Resident Evil' adaptation, captures the strange dichotomy of this popular video game series. It starts out terrifying, gradually becomes ridiculous, and totally gets away with that tonal shift." The picture is funny the way the games are funny, in the middle of the danger, and it never apologises for the switch.

What does not work is the last act. Tallerico wrote that "The final scenes also feel like they get away from Cregger a bit, clunkier than the rest of the movie. He seems to have way more fun setting up the chaos than being forced to conclude it". Hassenger heard the same wobble and judged it more gently, saying "Cregger only falters in the tail end, and it's more a sin of omission than a big misstep". There is also a subplot that never lands: the pregnancy hanging over Bryan is the one thread with no payoff, and the film never tests him on it.

On the numbers. Rotten Tomatoes has it at 96 percent from 300 critic reviews, with a 90 percent Popcornmeter from more than 10,000 verified ratings. Metacritic has a Metascore of 79 from 51 critic reviews, labelled Generally Favorable, plus a user score of 7.4 from 804 user ratings. IMDb has it at 7.6 out of 10 from 111,000 ratings. Letterboxd members have it at a weighted 3.75 from 1,599,062 ratings, with 776,456 written reviews.

That 3.75 is the one measure that disagrees with the room. Critics are close to unanimous and the large audience sample is not. Read it as a franchise split: the game-literate audience recognises what Cregger is doing, and the rest of the audience rates the ninety-five minutes.

At the time of writing there was no streaming offer for it in the US. It was still in theaters, and the only thing available to buy online was a Blu-ray. It has since become the highest-grossing Resident Evil film, with $148,990,438 domestic and $361,690,438 worldwide against a $75 million budget, and Wikipedia's summary of the same records lists it as the highest-rated video game adaptation on Rotten Tomatoes and Metacritic.

Watch it as a horror movie with a comedy running under it, not as a franchise instalment that owes you continuity. Cregger took a name with a bad history and built something with craft under it: a courier who cannot shoot, a farmhouse dog that splits open, a single night that keeps escalating.
