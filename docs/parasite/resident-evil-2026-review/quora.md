## The question this answers

Is the new Resident Evil (2026) worth watching? And where can I watch it in the US?

## Answer to paste

Yes, and it is worth the ninety-five minutes it asks for. This is the first film in the franchise built on horror instead of noise, and it is the only one you can watch without knowing the games.

Bryan Hodukavich is a medical courier whose shift is over. His girlfriend has just told him she is pregnant. He takes one more run anyway, up a mountain road in a snowstorm, in a car with no chains on the tires, carrying a Schedule A package he assumes is a human heart. Twenty minutes in he is on foot in a blizzard holding a shotgun he has never fired, and the film has laid out its grammar: a run of self-contained missions, each one a location to cross and a problem you get one attempt at.

The games are in the structure rather than the lore. There is an old typewriter in an upstairs room and a green plant in an abandoned car, which are a save point and a health pickup. That detail is what tells you what kind of film you are in. You do not need the games to enjoy it, but you will read the whole thing differently if you grew up with them.

Austin Abrams is the reason it works. RogerEbert.com's Brian Tallerico called the performance "a fantastic piece of work, the kind of physical acting that often doesn't get enough credit", and said Abrams does not turn Bryan into an action hero but an ordinary guy trying to do something important. The creature design is the image the film sells itself with, a dog that pushes its head through a barn wall and then splits open like a flower, and Tallerico compared the effect to the Large Marge sequence in Pee-Wee's Big Adventure.

The one weakness everyone agrees on is the last act. Tallerico wrote that the final scenes "feel like they get away from Cregger a bit", and The Guardian's Jesse Hassenger heard the same wobble and called it "more a sin of omission than a big misstep". The tone is the surprise: the picture is funny the way the games are funny, in the middle of the danger, and it never apologises for the switch.

On the numbers, Rotten Tomatoes has it at 96 percent from 300 critic reviews, with a 90 percent Popcornmeter from more than 10,000 verified ratings. Metacritic has it at 79 from 51 critic reviews, plus a user score of 7.4 from 804 user ratings. IMDb has it at 7.6 out of 10 from 111,000 ratings. Letterboxd members have it at a weighted 3.75 from 1,599,062 ratings, and that last number is the one worth understanding. Critics are close to unanimous and the audience is not, which reads as a franchise split: the game-literate crowd recognised what Cregger was doing, and everybody else rated ninety-five minutes.

For availability: at the time of writing it was still in US theaters with no streaming or rental offer, and the only thing available to buy online was a Blu-ray. It has since become the highest-grossing Resident Evil film, with $148,990,438 domestic and $361,690,438 worldwide, and Wikipedia's summary of the same records lists it as the highest-rated video game adaptation on Rotten Tomatoes and Metacritic. R, 95 minutes, directed by Zach Cregger.
