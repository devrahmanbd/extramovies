## The question this answers

Is Spider-Man: Brand New Day worth watching? And is there a streaming service for it yet, or do I have to wait for the disc?

## Answer to paste

Yes, it is worth watching, and the honest caveat is the runtime. Spider-Man: Brand New Day runs 145 minutes, and the climax is over before the characters are. If you can give it the time, the film inside is the most grown-up thing Marvel has made.

Here is the situation it starts from. Peter Parker erased himself from everyone's memory at the end of No Way Home to save the world. Four years on he lives alone in a rusted New York attic, takes small jobs under the mask, and nobody in the crowd cheers for him. When a body-hopping psychic outbreak begins eating the city, the Department of Damage Control contracts Spider-Man to stop it. So you get a superhero film where the hero is anonymous, tired and lonely, and the villain's trick is that the body it lands in usually belongs to an ordinary person.

The part that stays with me is the middle. Peter follows MJ around New York, watches her with a new boyfriend, and does not say anything. An act of a Spider-Man movie spent on a man being unable to open his mouth. It is also why I would go back to it, because it uses the one thing this franchise does not usually touch, which is consequence.

Tom Holland is 30 and playing a man rather than a boy, and the tiredness in his voice sounds real rather than acted. Zendaya gets the best material in the picture. MJ is a civilian with a career and a boyfriend and no idea that the man she is talking to in a coffee shop saved the world twice. The Guardian's Peter Bradshaw wrote that "Zendaya saves this film." Sadie Sink plays Jean Grey with a rewritten origin and a reason for what she does, and she is the most interesting new element Marvel has introduced in a while.

The complaints are about length and about franchise upkeep. The Los Angeles Times said the film "finds its nimble hero tangled by franchise upkeep," with Hulk, a new Black Widow and a radicalised Department of Damage Control all being fitted in ahead of Avengers: Doomsday. Variety's Owen Gleiberman called it "arduous, overly episodic, and half an hour too long."

On the numbers, Rotten Tomatoes has it at 90 percent from 439 critic reviews, with a 97 percent Popcornmeter from more than 25,000 verified ratings. Metacritic has it at 66 from 56 critic reviews, which was the lowest score any aggregator gave it. Letterboxd members have it at a weighted 4.03 from 4,580,007 ratings. Critics and audiences disagree about it, and the audience is on the warmer side.

On availability, there is no subscription service for it in the US yet. It is for rent and for purchase on Amazon Video, the Apple TV Store and Fandango At Home, and for rental on Google Play Movies and YouTube. The digital release was October 6, 2026, and the 4K Ultra HD, Blu-ray and DVD are dated December 15, 2026. PG-13, 145 minutes, directed by Destin Daniel Cretton.
