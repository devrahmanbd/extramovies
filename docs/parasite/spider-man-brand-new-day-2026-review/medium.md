## Title

Spider-Man: Brand New Day review: the sad one, the slow one, and the one worth it

## Subtitle

Destin Daniel Cretton made the most grown-up Marvel film ever and then gave it half an hour too much runtime.

---

## Copy to paste

Yes, Spider-Man: Brand New Day is worth watching, with one warning about the length. It is a bruised, quiet film about a man nobody knows. The sadness in it arrives late, the climax is over before the characters are, and both of those are facts about the cut rather than about the story.

The set-up is unusual for the franchise. Peter Parker erased himself from the world's memory at the end of No Way Home to save it. Four years later he lives alone in a rusted New York attic with a wall of screens and no friends. MJ and Ned are at MIT and do not know him. He works full-time as Spider-Man, which in this film means taking small jobs, wearing a mask, and going home to nobody. When a body-hopping psychic outbreak starts eating the city, the Department of Damage Control contracts him to stop it.

## A superhero film about a man who cannot speak

The act that surprised me is the one where Peter follows MJ around New York, watches her with a new boyfriend, and says nothing. That is an act of a Spider-Man movie spent on a man being unable to open his mouth. It is also the thing that made the film interesting to me, because it takes the franchise's one asset, Peter's gift for talking, and takes it away.

Tom Holland is 30 and playing a man rather than a boy. He stands differently in the mask and the tiredness in his voice sounds real rather than performed. The Guardian's Peter Bradshaw caught it as Holland being "back as the vexed webslinger in a sometimes shocking, occasionally sluggish new instalment."

## The real problem is padding

Here is the criticism that lands hardest. The Los Angeles Times put the finger on it, and the film's own running time confirmed it. Amy Nicholson wrote that the film "finds its nimble hero tangled by franchise upkeep," and pointed at the cause: the screenwriters add too much padding with their need to include Hulk, the new Black Widow and a radicalised Department of Damage Control, all of it in place before Avengers: Doomsday. Variety's Owen Gleiberman called the result "arduous, overly episodic, and half an hour too long."

There is a sharper version of the same complaint about the romance. Gleiberman put it as: "We want the relationship between Peter and MJ to be a journey, not a frozen piece of sad irony." The film stalls there for longer than the material can carry.

What nobody disputes is the tone. Cretton is trying to make a comic-book movie that is thoughtful and humane, and Gleiberman's summary of the failure is fair: "you feel how hard a movie is working to be those things, it tends to detract from the goal." The ambition is real, and so is the seam where the ambition shows.

## The body horror is played as a burden

The most adult thing a Spider-Man picture has attempted is the new powers. Peter cannot switch them off. His headaches are not a setup for a joke. The Guardian's Bradshaw described the change as a disquieting development, with "unprecedented new physical and mental health issues for Parker," and put it plainly: "He has headaches, and these appear to be taking our hero to what I can only describe as the David Cronenberg zone."

## The numbers

Rotten Tomatoes has the film at 90 percent from 439 critic reviews, with a 97 percent Popcornmeter from more than 25,000 verified ratings. Metacritic has a Metascore of 66 from 56 critic reviews, labelled Generally Favorable, plus a user score of 7.9 from 1,120 user ratings. IMDb has it at 7.9 out of 10 from 360,000 ratings. Letterboxd members have it at a weighted 4.03 from 4,580,007 ratings, with 2,006,580 written reviews and 55,000 fans following the film.

The critical panels split and the audience does not. Sixty-six is the lowest score any of the aggregators gave it, and 4.03 from 4.5 million Letterboxd members says the people who went had a good time. It is the biggest domestic and worldwide grosser of 2026, at $958,315,789 in the US and $2,516,315,789 worldwide.

In the US there is no subscription service yet. It is for rent and for purchase on Amazon Video, the Apple TV Store and Fandango At Home, and for rental on Google Play Movies and YouTube. The digital release was October 6, 2026, and the disc is dated December 15, 2026.

## The short version

It is the most grown-up film Marvel has made, and it is carried by two actors rather than by the plot. Gleiberman's summary was that the film is "more busy and self-serious than it is emotionally involving." He was close. The picture is busy, the emotion is real, and it arrives about a reel too late.
