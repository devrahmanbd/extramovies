## Title

Is The Odyssey worth watching? Our verdict on Nolan's IMAX epic

## Subtitle

The first film ever shot entirely on IMAX film. Vast, cold, and the rare three-hour blockbuster that earns its running time.

---

## Copy to paste

Yes, The Odyssey is worth watching, and it is the rare blockbuster that earns the three hours it asks for. Christopher Nolan's Greek epic is vast and strangely cold. Critics have almost nothing but praise for it, audiences mostly agree, and the one dissenting number points at the film's real weakness before you have seen a frame of it.

The format is the first thing to understand. This is the first feature film shot entirely on IMAX film, and the first feature-length commercial film released entirely in 70mm IMAX. You can feel it in the way a body feels heavy. The ships have weight, the horses strain, and when a wall comes down you feel the heat of it. The Guardian's Peter Bradshaw described "gasp-inducing, Imax-sized landscapes of loneliness shot by cinematographer Hoyte van Hoytema", and noted that he "avoids the sea's traditional cliched colour".

## It is not really about the monsters

Odysseus has been away from Ithaca for twenty years, and the island has been ruled by nobody since he left for Troy. Penelope holds the house together while the suitors eat her food and drink her wine. Their son Telemachus goes looking for his father. The film opens in a banquet hall with a man standing on a table telling the story of the Trojan Horse, and then it starts rearranging time. Distant past, recent past and present, shuffled like a deck of cards.

Bradshaw named the subject, and it is not the Cyclops. He called it a "colossal origin-myth story of postwar disillusion" and said the film "speaks to the generational pain of PTSD". Odysseus is not cursed. He is a man who cannot get home because of what he did in the war. That reframing is what makes the picture more than a list of set pieces.

Matt Damon plays Odysseus with a face that has been worn down to a mask. Bradshaw caught the technique exactly: "his boyish, almost cherubic face turned into a careworn mask of sadness". He is a trickster who keeps breaking his own code of honour, and the film plays that as a character flaw rather than as a wink. The film lets him be tired and out of his depth, which is rarer than it sounds in this genre.

## The war is the part that works

The sacking of Troy is not a triumph scene. Matt Zoller Seitz at RogerEbert.com used Penelope's anger at the patriarchal entitlement that lets the suitors strip Ithaca of its riches as his first example of the production serving the characters' feelings. Odysseus's guilt is the engine of the plot. He was not delayed by the gods. He was delayed by the war and by his refusal to look at what the war did.

The quiet stretches are better than the monster sequences, and the film knows it. The stretches of Penelope and Telemachus without Odysseus in the room, the long table, the suitors behaving badly, are the parts people remember afterwards.

## The coldness, and the number that disagrees

Guy Lodge at Variety headed his review "Christopher Nolan's Vast, Thrilling, Slightly Aloof Epic". His summary was that the film "thrills generously for the bulk of its near three-hour running time", but "it's never exactly moving". The picture keeps your eyes busy and your heart somewhere else. The famous feeling of a Nolan finale is missing, and while that is a choice, it is a choice the audience feels.

That 5.7 is the number to sit with. It is the only audience measure that dissents, and 3,346 ratings is enough to notice. Some of it is the 173-minute running time, and some of it is that the film rewards patience instead of forgiving it.

## Where to watch it

At the time of writing it was still in American cinemas, and there was no streaming or rental offer for it in the US. Universal lists it for digital on November 17, 2026, with 4K Ultra HD, Blu-ray and DVD on the same day, and rental on Apple TV and Prime Video. No subscription date has been announced.

The business explains the scale. It is the second highest-grossing film of 2026, at $624,964,305 domestic and $1,788,848,305 worldwide, and IMAX Corporation reported a record $52 million global opening weekend. The Guardian reported that it passed $1.1 billion to become Nolan's highest-grossing film, and that it later overtook Deadpool and Wolverine to become the highest-grossing R-rated film of all time.

## The short version

Nolan has made a war film about the end of war, with the best tools the industry has, and it earns the three hours it asks for. Lodge's objection is the right one and it is a small one: the film is a little too pleased with itself, a little too cool to let you in. His review called it Nolan's most personal picture since Oppenheimer and his least consoling, which is a defensible choice for a story about a man who cannot go home.
