## The question this answers

Where can I watch The Odyssey in the US, and is it worth the ticket? Is it still in cinemas?

## Answer to paste

It is worth the ticket, and at the time of writing it was still in American cinemas with no streaming or rental offer in the US. Universal lists it for digital on November 17, 2026, with 4K Ultra HD, Blu-ray and DVD on the same day, and rental on Apple TV and Prime Video. No subscription date has been announced. If you have a big screen and a cinema near you, this is the last few weeks it will be in theatres, and the picture deserves one.

The first thing to know is the format, because it changes what you are watching. This is the first feature film shot entirely on IMAX film, and the first feature-length commercial film released entirely in 70mm IMAX. You can feel it in the way a body feels heavy. The ships have weight, the horses strain, and when a wall comes down you feel the heat of it. The Guardian's Peter Bradshaw described "gasp-inducing, Imax-sized landscapes of loneliness" and noted that the cinematographer "avoids the sea's traditional cliched colour".

The second thing to know is that it is not mainly a monsters film. Odysseus has been away from Ithaca for twenty years. Penelope holds the house together while the suitors eat her food and drink her wine, their son Telemachus goes looking for his father, and the film opens with a man standing on a table telling the story of the Trojan Horse before it starts rearranging time. Bradshaw named the subject as postwar disillusion, and said the film "speaks to the generational pain of PTSD". Matt Damon plays a man who cannot get home because of what he did in the war, and Anne Hathaway's Penelope is furious and running the room while the men in it drink her wine.

What you might not enjoy is the temperature. Variety's Guy Lodge headed his review "Christopher Nolan's Vast, Thrilling, Slightly Aloof Epic", writing that the film "thrills generously for the bulk of its near three-hour running time" but is "never exactly moving". The picture keeps your eyes busy and your heart somewhere else.

On the numbers, Rotten Tomatoes has it at 94 percent from 515 critic reviews, with a 96 percent Popcornmeter from more than 25,000 verified ratings. Metacritic has it at 88 from 63 critic reviews, labelled Universal Acclaim, plus a user score of 5.7 from 3,346 user ratings. IMDb has it at 8.4 out of 10 from 534,000 ratings. Letterboxd members have it at a weighted 4.34 from 4,611,722 ratings. That 5.7 is the only measure that dissents and it is worth knowing about before you book, because it is usually the runtime people are reacting to.

It is the second highest-grossing film of 2026, and it passed $1.1 billion to become Nolan's highest-grossing film. R for violence and some language, 173 minutes, directed by Christopher Nolan.
