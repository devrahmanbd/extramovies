## Title

Is Toy Story 5 worth watching? The Jessie sequel verdict

## Subtitle

The best animation in the business, the right idea for 2026, and a script that walks back to where it started.

## Copy to paste

Yes, Toy Story 5 is worth watching. The animation is still the best in the industry. The idea belongs to the year it arrived in. The lead is a new one and she is good. The script is the problem, and the script is where a 7.4 out of 10 comes from.

Andrew Stanton took over a franchise he co-wrote twenty-nine years ago and did something the series had never done: he handed the picture to a supporting character. Joan Cusack's Jessie has been in these films since Toy Story 2, a cowgirl with a broken record and a wound that never closed. She was Emily's toy, Emily grew up and left her, and Jessie has been carrying that ever since. In Toy Story 5 she is the lead, and she is playing a woman with a job to do.

## A film about a kid who still plays with toys

The setup is the sharpest thing here. Bonnie is eight and still plays with toys, which in 2026 makes her the odd one out. The other children have screens, so the other children have friends, and Bonnie does not. Her parents solve it with a frog-shaped tablet called Lilypad, voiced by Greta Lee. Within fifteen minutes Bonnie has playdates. She also has a group chat in which the other children ridicule her for still clinging to her old toys, and that is the trap the whole picture is built on.

Jessie decides the tablet is the problem and starts a campaign to get Bonnie back to being a child who plays. The complication, and the thing that makes the film smarter than it looks, is that Lily can also be right. A friend you make through a screen is a different kind of friend from a friend you share a space with. Variety's Owen Gleiberman named the theme in his review: "a new theme emerges that's hauntingly and movingly of its own time: the disappearance of play." It is the first Toy Story film with a subject that belongs to the decade it was made in rather than to the era it grew out of.

## Where the script gives up

Two things weaken it, and both are structural.

The first is repetition. The first half sets up the tablet problem, the sleepover, the group chat, the toys going into storage and the farmhouse. The second half re-sets up the tablet problem at a larger scale. The plot moves in circles and lands where it started, and the film spends its best asset, Jessie, on the detour.

The second is that the big idea gets abandoned. Peter Bradshaw at The Guardian wrote that the film "even calamitously loses its nerve with its own big idea." The theme the franchise has been built on, a toy outliving the child who loved it, "feels underpowered" in a picture that spends most of its running time on a tablet. For a film about what happens when a child grows up, the grief is doing very little work.

The critics split on exactly this line. The Guardian saw "the unblemished sheen of a brand new smartphone. But at heart, it has gone dead." Deadline's Damon Wise called it "the biggest family movie of the year," which is true about the surface and not about the second half. Daniels was blunter: this film "doesn't take many risks: it's your prototypically beautifully rendered movie tackling a heady subject in the safest possible manner."

## The numbers, and where to watch

Rotten Tomatoes has the film at 92 percent from 331 critic reviews, with a 94 percent Popcornmeter from more than 10,000 verified ratings. Metacritic has a Metascore of 73 from 55 critic reviews, labelled Generally Favorable, plus a user score of 7.0 from 365 user ratings. IMDb has it at 7.3 out of 10 from 102,000 ratings. Letterboxd members have it at a weighted 3.62 from more than 1.2 million ratings, with 515,210 written reviews.

Metacritic's 73 sits below Rotten Tomatoes and IMDb, and Letterboxd's 3.62 is the same temperature in audience form. A 3.62 on the five-point scale, from a crowd this size, is a good film's verdict on a film nearly everyone went to see.

It is on Disney Plus in the US. Rental and purchase are on Amazon Video, the Apple TV Store and Fandango At Home. The certificate is PG and the run time is 102 minutes.

## The short version

Take it for the craft and the theme. The animation is immaculate, the subject is right, and Jessie is a lead worth the ticket. Leave the expectation of a brave script at the door. This is a good 102 minutes, and it is good because the animation carries it.
