## The question this answers

Is Toy Story 5 worth watching? Also, if you have kids, is it the kind of film that holds up for them, or does it feel like a marketing exercise for a tablet?

## Answer to paste

Yes, it is worth watching, and the reason is narrower than most people expect. Toy Story 5 is a gorgeous 102-minute Pixar comedy with a genuinely new subject and a genuinely new lead. Its script takes the same detour twice and never quite pays off the argument it starts. So the verdict depends on what you are buying.

The new part is real. Joan Cusack plays Jessie, who has been in these films since Toy Story 2, a cowgirl who was given away as a child and never got over it. In this one she is the lead, with a job to do and a point of view. The other new part is the antagonist. Bonnie is eight and still plays with toys, which in 2026 makes her the odd one out, so her parents buy her a frog-shaped tablet called Lilypad. It works instantly, she has playdates in fifteen minutes, and then she lands in a group chat where the other kids mock her for still playing with her old toys. That is the whole film in one situation, and it is sharper than the premise of any previous Toy Story.

The failure is in the structure. The first half sets up the tablet problem, the sleepover, the group chat, the toys being put into storage and the farmhouse. The second half sets the same problem up again at a bigger scale, and the plot ends exactly where it started. The theme the franchise has been built on since Toy Story 2, a toy outliving the child who loved it, gets very little screen time in a film mostly about a tablet. The Guardian's Peter Bradshaw put it as the film losing its nerve with its own big idea. RogerEbert.com's Robert Daniels said it "doesn't take many risks."

On the numbers, Rotten Tomatoes has it at 92 percent from 331 critic reviews, Metacritic has it at 73 from 55 critic reviews, and Letterboxd members have it at a weighted 3.62 from more than 1.2 million ratings. That last number is the one to sit with. It is a good crowd's verdict on a film nearly everyone went to see.

For the kids question: the animation is the best in the business and the new toys are funny (Smarty Pants the toilet trainer, Snappy the camera, Atlas the GPS hippo). It is a safe film, which is not the same thing as a bad one. A family with younger children will have a good time. A family with older children, or anyone who goes to Pixar for the writing, will notice the loop.

Where to watch: Disney Plus in the US, with rental and purchase on Amazon Video, the Apple TV Store and Fandango At Home. PG, 102 minutes, directed by Andrew Stanton.
