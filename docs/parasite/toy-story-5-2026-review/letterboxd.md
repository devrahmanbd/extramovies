Toy Story 5 (2026)
Dir. Andrew Stanton | 102 min | PG | US release June 19, 2026

Worth it, especially for a family, and mostly because the animation is still the best in the business. Andrew Stanton hands the franchise to Joan Cusack's Jessie and makes the antagonist a frog-shaped tablet called Lilypad. The subject is new too: Bonnie is eight and still plays with toys, which in 2026 makes her the odd one out, so the other kids have screens and therefore friends, and she does not. The film asks what that does to a kid who is still interested in the physical world, and for about an hour it asks it well.

Cusack carries it. Jessie was a supporting player for twenty-seven years and she plays the lead as a cowgirl with a broken record and a job to do. Tim Allen's Buzz does something the character has never done, which is be nervous about asking her out. Tom Hanks's Woody is charming and this film does less with him. Greta Lee plays Lily with a cheerfulness that makes the film's own argument harder than it should be. Mykal-Michelle Harris plays Blaze, who is Bonnie's answer.

What holds it back is that the film does not trust the idea. The script repeats itself. The first half sets up the tablet, the sleepover, the group chat, the toys going into storage, the farmhouse. The second half re-sets up the tablet in a bigger way, and the plot lands exactly where it started, having spent the film's best asset on the detour. The grown-ups are written small. Woody is balding, and the running jokes about his weight and his shiny bald spot stay jokes and never turn into anything.

The sharpest criticism of it, from The Guardian, is that the film "even calamitously loses its nerve with its own big idea." The theme the whole franchise rests on, a toy outliving the child who loved it, feels underpowered in a picture that spends most of its running time on a tablet. For a film about what happens when a child grows up, the grief is doing very little work.

The audience numbers tell you how many people had the same afternoon. Rotten Tomatoes has it at 92 percent from 331 critic reviews, with a 94 percent Popcornmeter from more than 10,000 verified ratings. Metacritic has it at 73 from 55 critic reviews, labelled Generally Favorable, and a user score of 7.0 from 365 ratings. IMDb has it at 7.3 from 102,000 ratings. Letterboxd members have it at a weighted 3.62 from more than 1.2 million ratings. That is a good crowd's verdict on a film nearly everyone went to.

On Disney Plus in the US. Rental and purchase on Amazon Video, the Apple TV Store and Fandango At Home.

This is a good 102 minutes, and it is good because the animation carries it and the theme is right, not because the script is brave. That the latest Toy Story is still this watchable is not a small thing.
