## Title

Digger review: Cruise is extraordinary, the film shouts the rest

## Subtitle

Alejandro González Iñárritu made a loud picture about a loud man. The man is the reason to go.

---

## Copy to paste

Yes, Digger is worth watching, for Tom Cruise and for the images of a world coming apart that the rest of the film keeps interrupting. Iñárritu's satire puts Cruise in prosthetics as an ageing Texas oil tycoon who talks the White House into solving a crisis he caused. The performance is the best thing in the picture. The film around it is loud, smug and slow.

Here is the premise. Digger Rockwell is an old oilman in a mansion decorated with antlers and stuffed animals, waking beside a cat that is disintegrating by the hour. He is roused by a call from the Arctic. An explosion at one of his rigs has freed a vast ice floe, many times the size of Manhattan, drifting toward the coast of Norway, and where it lands it will be a nuclear strike in slow motion. Empire's Olly Richards wrote the floe's name into the review: "an iceberg named Judy is on her way to decimate Scandinavia."

Digger denies the danger, then denies it harder. The president, played by John Goodman, and his cabinet gather, along with Digger's chief scientist Dr. Ganesh, played by Riz Ahmed, who has been warning him for years. Digger takes over the meeting anyway, lambasting the president for his reluctance to take responsibility for a situation his own drilling created, and then he tells the United Nations the truth, which is that renewables will not save anyone and that the world owes him.

## The performance is the reason to go

Cruise plays Digger under a bald cap, jowls, a Texas drawl and a great deal of Kazu Hiro prosthetic work, and he does it without a wink. Olly Richards wrote that "Cruise emerges with a win, reminding us how riveting he can be as an actor, not just as a movie star", and that the role "demands fearless commitment, which he certainly gives". Robert Daniels at RogerEbert.com saw the same thing from the other side of the screen, noting that "Cruise's performance, consequently, moves with slippery intensity". Deadline's Pete Hammond was the warmest of the reviews on record, noting that "Cruise has never done a role like this one".

## The images are the second reason

Emmanuel Lubezki shot it and Dennis Gassner designed it, and they build a world that is tipping, warped and gorgeous. The palette does half the persuading, with the main run of the story in a putrid grey and everything around it in full colour. Peter Bradshaw at The Guardian put the film's real value in those pictures: "some dreamlike and disturbing images, distorted and bulging from the screen".

## The volume problem

Every theme arrives as a monologue delivered at full volume with the camera tilting up. Daniels, who gave it two stars out of four, found the dialogue "tellingly didactic" and its actors "scream their lines during overcooked shouting matches, as though the louder they verbalize any given idea, the more it'll mean". He also wrote that the film "is devoid of substance", and closed on it as "a sneering premise awaiting a drop of humanism".

Bradshaw called it "an exasperatingly shallow, laborious, self-congratulatory satire with a trick up its sleeve, featuring very good actors giving not very good performances". Richards, who was kinder, still found the film unable to stop admiring itself, and named the chief problem: "it appears more concerned with itself than with the audience". He also noticed that Hüller, Goodman and Plemons "each play a single note", and that the film, "for a film about the complexity of mankind, shows scant interest in actual people."

## The numbers

Rotten Tomatoes has the Tomatometer at 53 percent from 333 reviews and the Popcornmeter at 76 percent from more than 1,000 verified ratings. The platform's critics consensus calls the film "a bold swing of the shovel by Tom Cruise and Alejandro González Iñárritu that misses as often as it strikes gold". Metacritic has a metascore of 46 from 60 critic reviews, labelled Mixed or Average, with a user score of 7.2 from 268 ratings. Letterboxd members have it at a member average of 3.91 from 555,194 ratings and 288,387 written reviews.

That gap is the story. Critics and the paying audience are looking at two different movies, and the one the audience is enjoying is Cruise.

At the time of writing it was still in US cinemas, with no streaming, rental or purchase offer available in the United States. It opened on October 2, 2026.

## The short version

Yes, for Cruise, who does something with a face full of latex that no one else in the business could do, and for a set of images that make the last twenty years of news feel like a weather forecast. Iñárritu made a loud picture about a loud man, and the loud man is the reason to go.
