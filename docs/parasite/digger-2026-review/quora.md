## The question this answers

Is Digger (2026) worth watching? And where can I watch it in the US, because it does not seem to be on any streaming service?

## Answer to paste

Yes, for Tom Cruise, and for the images. The film around him is loud, smug and slow, and the honest way to describe it is that critics and audiences are watching two different movies.

The premise: Digger Rockwell is an old oilman in a mansion decorated with antlers and stuffed animals, waking beside a cat that is disintegrating by the hour. An explosion at one of his rigs has freed a vast ice floe, many times the size of Manhattan, drifting toward the coast of Norway, and where it lands it will be a nuclear strike in slow motion. Empire's Olly Richards wrote the floe's name into the review: "an iceberg named Judy is on her way to decimate Scandinavia". Cruise plays him under a bald cap, jowls, a Texas drawl and a great deal of Kazu Hiro prosthetic work, and it is extraordinary. Richards wrote that "Cruise emerges with a win, reminding us how riveting he can be as an actor, not just as a movie star", and RogerEbert.com's Robert Daniels noted that the performance "moves with slippery intensity".

The other reason to go is the look of it. Emmanuel Lubezki shot it and Dennis Gassner designed it, and the world they build is tipping, warped and gorgeous, mostly in a putrid grey with everything around it in full colour. The Guardian put the film's real value in "some dreamlike and disturbing images, distorted and bulging from the screen".

The complaint is the volume. Every theme gets a monologue at full volume with the camera tilting up. Daniels, who gave it two stars out of four, found the dialogue "tellingly didactic" and the actors "scream their lines during overcooked shouting matches". The Guardian's Peter Bradshaw called it "an exasperatingly shallow, laborious, self-congratulatory satire with a trick up its sleeve". If you want a satire that argues its case rather than announcing it, this is not it.

The numbers tell you where to stand. Rotten Tomatoes has it at 53 percent from 333 critic reviews, with a Popcornmeter at 76 percent from more than 1,000 verified ratings. Metacritic has it at 46 from 60 critic reviews, labelled Mixed or Average, plus a user score of 7.2 from 268 ratings. Letterboxd members have it at 3.91 from 555,194 ratings.

On availability: at the time of writing it was still in US cinemas, and JustWatch and TMDB could not find a streaming, rental or purchase offer for the United States. Tickets were on sale through Cinemark, Harkins Theatres and Marcus Theatres.

R for pervasive language, 129 minutes, directed by Alejandro González Iñárritu, released in the US on October 2, 2026.
