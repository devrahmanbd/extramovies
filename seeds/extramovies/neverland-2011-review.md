Yes, Neverland is worth watching, but mostly for the actors. The 2011 Syfy miniseries is a two-part prequel to J.M. Barrie's Peter Pan, written and directed by Nick Willing, and the programme itself runs 170 minutes, the two 85-minute parts TMDB lists, while the Syfy broadcast ran to the four hours Variety's credits block records. Rhys Ifans plays Jimmy Hook with a bank robber's calm, Anna Friel plays the pirate captain Elizabeth Bonny like she has been at sea since birth, and Bob Hoskins turns up one last time in the role of Smee. Then the script puts its feet up. Rotten Tomatoes has the miniseries at 57 percent from 14 critic reviews, Metacritic at 59 from 14 critic reviews, both checked on October 22, 2026.

> **Spoiler-free verdict:** Watch it for Ifans and Friel, skip it if you want a Peter Pan that moves. ## What Neverland is about

London, 1906. Jimmy Hook (Rhys Ifans) runs a gang of street thieves who lift a glowing orb from an antique shop, and the orb takes Hook and most of the boys to another world. Peter Pan (Charlie Rowe), the lookout left behind, thinks his friends are dead, carries the orb home, and is told by Dr. Richard Fludd (Charles Dance) that they are alive and unreachable. Fludd, the royal alchemist for Queen Elizabeth I, built the orb, and Neverland is a planet at the four corners of the universe where time stands still, so a Victorian pickpocket and an eighteenth-century pirate captain can share the same forest.

Hook is not there by accident. He is there to steal the mineral dust that gives the tree spirits their flight, for Elizabeth Bonny (Anna Friel), who has a crew and no way home. Peter, Fox and the other Lost Boys are captured, escape, and are taken in by the Kaw tribe, whose chief's daughter Aaya is played by Q'orianka Kilcher. The tree spirits are led by Tinker Bell, voiced by Keira Knightley, with Charlotte Atkinson playing her on screen. Hook and Peter both want the orb back to their own century, and they want it for reasons neither of them will say out loud for most of the run.

## Cast and performances

Ifans is the reason to sit through it. Variety's Brian Lowry called him the best thing in a production he found thin: "As for the cast, Ifans' ruthless, gimlet-eyed Jimmy is the clear highlight, while the cameos include a drive-by Charles Dance and Bob Hoskins as Smee, reprising the role he played in Steven Spielberg's 'Hook.'" Friel is the other live wire. Mary McNamara in the Los Angeles Times called her "a deliciously bad pirate queen" and her villainy "first-class".

Charlie Rowe, who plays Peter, is the weakest of the leads, and the Chicago Tribune's review put it bluntly: "Knightly is wasted, Rowe barely registers and Ifans seems as bored as I was." The last clause is the part worth arguing with. Ifans is the only actor here who looks like he is enjoying himself, and Rowe plays Peter as a boy who has been handed a job he is too young and too polite for.

## What works

The production design. Parallel Films shot it in Swords, County Dublin, and in Genoa, and the Neverland it built is a forest planet with glowing tree spirits, a giant spider and a ten-legged crocodile. The one problem is the name the script gives the fairy dust, mineral dust, which sounds like a works order.

The premise. Neverland as a planet rather than an island is a real idea, and it pays off in the strangest image in the miniseries: a Victorian criminal, a pirate queen from another century and a group of street children sharing one forest because none of them can age there.

Hoskins. He was 68 when he filmed it and it was his last television role, and Smee here is the same Smee from Hook with a little more wear on him. It is a small thing in a 170-minute programme and it is one of the few moments that feels like someone made a choice rather than a note.

## What doesn't

The running time. The programme is 170 minutes and the Syfy broadcast ran to four hours, which is the gap every English-language reviewer complained about in different words. The Chicago Tribune: "Over the four hours, which could easily be edited to two, writer/director Nick Willing tediously shows us how Jimmy becomes Capt. Hook and Peter adds the Pan, and it feels pretty much like reading the list in the previous paragraph."

The effects. The flight scenes are uneven, with CGI beasties standing in for the real thing, and a Peter Pan where the characters sometimes fall is a difficult premise.

The telegraphing. Lowry again: "Familiarity with Peter Pan actually works against the mini as much as for it, since many of the little twists are so telegraphed." A twist the audience sees coming is not a twist, it is a rumour.

The Kaw tribe. The miniseries dresses its tree-spirit neighbours in a Plains stereotype, with a chief who speaks in a register nobody else in the film uses, and casts Latino and Indigenous performers as a lost tribe who exist to worship a forest. A Letterboxd member, Jack, writing on June 28, 2018, called the miniseries "occasionally racist and cheap looking" and said the redface in the Kaw scenes was "too much redface for the twenty first century." It is the single biggest mark against the show, and no amount of good casting in the lead roles puts it right.

## What audiences say

The gap between critics and viewers is narrow here, and the middling verdict is the consensus. Rotten Tomatoes has the miniseries at 57 percent from 14 critic reviews, and the series page carries no audience score at all. Metacritic's metascore is 59 from 14 critic reviews, labelled "Mixed or Average", with the critics split 36 percent positive, 64 percent mixed and none negative, and a user score of 6.8 from 17 ratings. Letterboxd's member average is 3.05 from 2,206 ratings, with 170 minutes of running time on the page. TMDB has it at 6.2 from 216 votes. Every figure in this section was checked on October 22, 2026.

The audience did turn up. The Hollywood Reporter's Philiana Ng reported on December 6, 2011 that the first part "drew 2.6 million total viewers from 9-11 p.m. on Sunday evening, up 5 percent form [sic] the cable network's most recent miniseries Alice (an update on Alice in Wonderland) from 2009", with nearly a million viewers in the adults 18-49 demo.

## Verdict

Is Neverland worth watching? Yes, for Ifans, for Friel, and for the fact that somebody took a nursery story and gave it a planet, a pirate captain and a treachery nobody had thought to build. It is also much longer than it needs to be, its effects wobble, its twists are telegraphed, and its treatment of its indigenous characters belongs to a worse decade. Variety's Brian Lowry, the harshest of the three daily newspapers that reviewed it, called the miniseries "damnation with faint sprinkles of fairy dust." He was close. The 57 percent on Rotten Tomatoes and the 3.05 on Letterboxd are the score for a production that got the look right and the length wrong.
