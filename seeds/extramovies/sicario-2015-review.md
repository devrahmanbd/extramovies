Sicario is a war movie where the war has no rules and the good cop is the only one who read them. Denis Villeneuve's 2015 border thriller follows FBI agent Kate Macer (Emily Blunt), recruited onto a task force led by Josh Brolin's Matt Graver, whose real instrument is Benicio del Toro's Alejandro, a sicario with a private agenda. Rotten Tomatoes has it at 91% from 279 critics with an 8.1/10 average, Metacritic scored 82 out of 100 for universal acclaim, and IMDb users rate it 7.7/10 from more than half a million votes, checked October 24, 2026. Taylor Sheridan's screenwriting debut launched a run of American frontier dramas, and this remains the sharpest of them.

> **Spoiler-free verdict:** A tense, morally ugly masterpiece of a thriller. Sicario earns its dread with real tradecraft, Blunt's straight-faced honesty, and Del Toro's quiet devastation. Not a comfortable watch, a great one.

## What Sicario is about

Kate Macer's raid on an Arizona house uncovers bodies in the walls, and the men who brief her afterwards, Brolin's Graver and the smooth-talking government man Victor Garber plays, pull her across the border on a mission that never explains itself. What she thinks is a capture operation is a pressure campaign against a cartel, run with techniques the FBI cannot touch, and the operative who makes it work is Alejandro, a former lawyer turned instrument of revenge. The film plays each operation as a procedure that slowly reveals its own lie, from the Juarez convoy sequence to the tunnel assault that closes the story.

The film premiered in competition for the Palme d'Or at Cannes 2015, and Sheridan's screenplay, his first, became the template for his later work.

## Cast and performances

Blunt carries the film's ethics in her face. She plays Macer as competent and uncorrupted, the rare action lead whose refusal to accept the rules is treated as the point rather than a flaw to fix, and The Guardian's Peter Bradshaw noted how she "brazens out any possible absurdity with great acting focus and front" in a role that could have been a plot device. Del Toro answers her with almost no dialogue: Alejandro works through stillness, and the scenes where he does nothing are the ones people remember. Brolin's Graver is the cheerful face of the machine, and the supporting bench of Jeffrey Donovan, Jon Bernthal, and Daniel Kaluuya keeps the operations credible.

## What works

Everything technical. Roger Deakins' photography turns the border into a landscape of dust and heat haze, the staging of each set piece lets geography read without a wasted cut, and the Jofannsson score holds the tension without release. The structure is also the argument: Villeneuve films the war on drugs from the perspective of the person who loses it, and the film's most violent act is its quietest. Rotten Tomatoes' consensus still reads as the best summary: "Led by outstanding work from Emily Blunt and Benicio del Toro, Sicario is a taut, tightly wound thriller with much more on its mind than attention-getting set pieces."

## What doesn't

The plot machinery occasionally uses Kate. Several of the film's key turns happen to her rather than because of her, and critics at the time argued the character is engineered into powerlessness for the theme's sake. The third act's body count also trades in a bleakness some viewers find punishing rather than clarifying. It is a deliberate cost: the film wants you uncomfortable, and not everyone wants that from a thriller they paid for.

## What audiences say

The audience verdict is strong and stable: IMDb holds 7.7/10 from 536,000 votes, and RT's verified audience reviews run to repeated rewatches with the Juarez sequence the most cited scene on the board. SlashFilm ranks it among Villeneuve's best on the Tomatometer, and the film's afterlife is the argument in its favor: it launched Sheridan's career, spawned a sequel duology, and remains the reference point for modern cartel cinema. TMDB has it at 7.4 from 9,999 votes, checked October 24, 2026.

## Verdict

Is Sicario worth watching? Yes, if you want your thrillers heavy. Villeneuve stages a procedural that turns into a moral horror story without changing tone once, Blunt and Del Toro give the decade its best pairing of leads, and Sheridan's debut script wastes nothing. It is a film about a war that consumes the people sent to fight it, and it never pretends otherwise.
