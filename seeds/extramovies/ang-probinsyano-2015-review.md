Ang Probinsyano is worth watching for Coco Martin and for what it accidentally became, which is a seven-year argument between Philippine television and the Philippine National Police. The ABS-CBN action drama ran in Filipino from September 28, 2015 to August 12, 2022, nine seasons and more than 1,600 episodes, and it held the top of the national ratings for three years running and is still counted among the most-watched series in Philippine television. It is also long, lumpy, melodramatic and a little absurd, and the middle seasons are a slog of shootouts and courtroom beats that earned the show more extensions than the writing deserved. Every ratings figure in this review was checked on October 22, 2026.

## What Ang Probinsyano is about

Coco Martin plays two men, the twins Ador and Cardo Dalisay. Ador is a respected CIDG officer. Cardo is a SAF trooper who likes the solitude of the mountains in Botolan. Ador is killed on a mission betrayed by a colleague, and Cardo is told to take his brother's place: his name, his work, his family, including a grandmother he had resented for assuming she abandoned him. That is the engine, and it runs for nearly seven years.

PEP.ph's James Patrick Anarcon described the arrangement when the pilot aired in September 2015: the teleserye turns on Ador, "a reponsible [sic] policeman who will be killed in a mission he is trying to solve", and Cardo takes over the mission and the mystery behind it. The pilot was directed by Malu Sevilla and Avel Sunpongco. Martin also served as the show's creative consultant, credited under his birth name, Rodel Nacianceno, and did most of his own stunts.

The story then breaks into five long arcs. The Syndicate Arc (2015-2017) has Cardo working criminal cases inside the CIDG. The Rebellion and Terrorism Arc (2017-2018) puts him undercover inside Pulang Araw, the Red Sun, the armed group he infiltrates and eventually joins. The Political Arc (2018-2019) is about Vendetta, the vigilantes Cardo has ended up with, getting into national politics. The Crime and Corruption Arc (2019-2021) has him back in uniform and up against Lily Ann Cortez (Lorna Tolentino), a First Lady running a drug syndicate behind the president's back. The International Arc (2021-2022) is Task Force Agila, the name the unit takes, heading north and then into the palace. The series is billed internationally as Brothers, which is the English name the databases and streaming services list it under.

## Cast and performances

Martin is the series, and everyone in the country knew it. He trained for the role, attended police flag-raising ceremonies, and by the end had become a political figure in his own right. The Philippine Daily Inquirer's Marinel R. Cruz interviewed him for the show's first anniversary in October 2016 and got this: "He was one of the show biz personalities I really idolized. I feel honored to be able to touch people's lives through something that FPJ created for the big screen." Cruz also got Susan Roces, Fernando Poe Jr.'s widow, playing Lola Flora, on the strangest moment in the show: "He was Cardo's twin brother. It felt real to me. I considered Ador and Cardo my own family."

The PNP fight, and the politics around it, is the most consequential thing the series did. In 2018 the police chief Oscar Albayalde complained that his fictional counterpart had been drawn as a villain, and the force ordered units to stop letting the production use police personnel, vehicles, firearms and buildings. The Philippine Daily Inquirer reported the government's position from both ends: Interior Secretary Eduardo Año said "It is just unfair to our good policemen in general who are doing their jobs properly and with devotion, even to the extent of putting their lives on the line," and Assistant Interior Secretary Jonathan Malaya said the department would "seriously consider filing legal action and imposing sanctions, including the prohibition on the use of PNP uniforms, properties and even using the acronym of the organization." The same paper gave politicians on both sides the floor. Sen. Panfilo Lacson, a former police chief, sided with Albayalde: "Given how so many people watch it ... I think, to say the least, it's unfair to the PNP to be portrayed liked that because they are not like that." Sen. Grace Poe, the daughter of Fernando Poe Jr., went the other way: "How the people view the police is based on the actions of the real police they see on the streets, and not on the acting of fictional cops they watch on screen." She put the value of the show in a line the police chief did not want to hear: "despite the wrongdoings happening around us, there are cops like Cardo, and his team, who will always fight for what is right and just."

The Inquirer's own NOLISOLI column was harder on the apology Coco Martin made to the force, and harder still on the force. Its verdict: "The apology from Coco Martin a.k.a. SPO2 Cardo Dalisay was unnecessary." The column asked the question the rest of the country was asking: "What was he sorry for? For starring in a high-rating show that accurately reflects corruption in the police force?"

## What works

The topicality. The Cebu Daily News's Malou Guanzon Apalisok made the case for the series as argument rather than escapism in November 2018: "Ang Probinsiyano [sic] is a social commentary of our times because the stories depict real happenings and real people. Powerful politicians and high-ranking police officials linked to illegal drug trade and other criminal activities are in fact the storyline and angle of many news headlines day in and day out." She had the number behind her: the series took a 47.2 percent national rating on Kantar's count in 2018, the second-highest figure in Philippine television history.

The scale of it. Apalisok: "Ang Probinsiyano [sic] has remained on top of the heap for three years in a row, even breaking its own viewership record." Nine seasons, a finale that drew a record 536,000-plus concurrent YouTube viewers, and a run on free-to-air television, cable and satellite that carried the show to 41 African countries through StarTimes.

The action. Martin worked with the action star Cesar Montano on the pilot, and Cruz asked him about it: "Our action scenes were difficult to do, but I didn't feel tired because I learned so much from working with him." The show is a genuine action series in a country where the action genre had been thinned out in the cinemas, and it is the reason the industry brought veteran stunt performers and out-of-work actors back to work.

Roces. Lola Flora is a grandmother, a gangster's widow and an audience surrogate at the same time, and Roces played her with the specific gravity that made the show's family scenes work.

## What doesn't

The sprawl. Nine seasons is too many for one engine, and the show kept expanding long after the writing had anything new to say. By the eighth and ninth seasons the episodes were running 22 to 33 minutes rather than the 35 to 45 of the earlier years, and a show that has already used up the deaths of most of its own unit is padding.

The censor run-ins. In August 2016 Rappler reported that the Movie and Television Review and Classification Board had written to ABS-CBN over a scene in the July 25 episode, in which Cardo stops to help Ella with a flat tyre in the rain, noting that the episode carried a strong parental guidance rating "despite the presence of what may be considered 'sexually suggestive' shots in the subject depiction". On July 21, 2017 the Municipal Council of Arayat forwarded a resolution to the provincial board demanding that the producers apologise for depicting the town "as a haven and breeding ground for terrorists and hoodlums", and ABS-CBN answered that the show "made no reference to the people of Arayat as rebels, nor was there any scene that was shot in Arayat", a line the Philippine Daily Inquirer carried on July 24, 2017. In July 2019 an episode depicting the rape of policewomen by the character Bungo, played by Baron Geisler, brought a warning from the police that the memorandum of understanding between the force and the show could be rescinded, as the Inquirer reported that July.

The tone. A drug-war police procedural and a family melodrama about a grandmother run in the same 35 minutes, and the writing oscillates between them without acknowledging the switch. This is a Filipino audience complaint rather than a critic's, but the ratings record shows the audience voted with their feet after 2018, and the show's own decision to keep going anyway is the most damning fact about it.

## What audiences say

Kantar Media's national television ratings, the standard measure for a Philippine teleserye, give the cleanest numbers here. The pilot on September 28, 2015 scored 41.6 percent, the highest rating for any pilot episode in the country since nationwide ratings began in 2009. The peak came on October 4, 2018, at 47.2 percent, the second-highest rating in Philippine television history behind the 2009 to 2013 serial May Bukas Pa. The finale on August 12, 2022 scored 16.6 percent and topped the national ratings chart.

The most recent national figure is AGB Nielsen's. PEP.ph's James Patrick Anarcon reported on February 2, 2022 that on February 1, 2022 the series registered a TV rating of 11.8 percent across ABS-CBN's television platforms, TV5 and A2Z on free TV and the Kapamilya Channel on cable, beating GMA-7's I Left My Heart in Sorsogon at 11.2 percent. It was the first night the series had won the ratings since June 2020, when it returned on the Kapamilya Channel after ABS-CBN's broadcast franchise was taken away by Congress.

The English-language numbers are thin because the series barely has any. TMDB has it at 6.6 from 36 votes, the smallest sample of anything in this review. Rotten Tomatoes has no page for the series, Metacritic does not list it, and Letterboxd's Ang Probinsyano page belongs to Fernando Poe Jr.'s 1996 film rather than the television series. Every figure in this section was checked on October 22, 2026, and IMDb is bot-blocked, so none of its numbers are quoted here.

## Where to watch Ang Probinsyano

In the United States the practical answer is that you need patience, and the title you will need is the other one. Netflix acquired the series in 2019 and streamed its first season under the international title Brothers, a change the Philippine Star reported at the time under the headline "'Ang Probinsyano' nasa Netflix na... pero iba ang pamagat". JustWatch lists Brothers (2015) for the United States as not available for streaming, read on October 22, 2026, and TMDB's US watch-provider list for the title is empty.

In the Philippines it is a domestic broadcast property. It aired on ABS-CBN's free-to-air network until March 13, 2020, moved to the Kapamilya Channel on cable from June 15, 2020, and later ran on A2Z Channel 11 and TV5, which is how it reached 41 African countries through StarTimes from June 29, 2021. TMDB's Philippine watch providers list iWant, ABS-CBN's free ad-supported service, as the on-demand home for it. Regular livestreams on YouTube began on July 14, 2020 with a debut stream that reached 56,000 live viewers, and the finale drew more than 536,000.

## Verdict

Is Ang Probinsyano worth watching? Yes, and the case for it rests on reach rather than writing. It is one of the most-watched series in Philippine television, it is why a generation of Filipinos under forty knows the name Cardo Dalisay, and from 2018 into 2020 it made the question of what the police do to poor people a nightly argument on the national airwaves. The writing is not the reason to watch it. The middle is a slog, the later seasons are shorter and looser, and the show would rather shout than subdue. The Philippine Daily Inquirer's NOLISOLI column had the right frame for the whole thing when it called Coco Martin's apology to the police unnecessary: the show had done what a police drama does, which is show the police. What you make of seven years of that is the reason to start.

## Spoiler warning: the ending of Ang Probinsyano

The final episode is titled Mission Accomplished, and Cardo Dalisay finishes what Task Force Agila started. The unit goes after Renato (John Arcilla) and Lucio (Raymond Bagatsing), the warlord who has been hiding in the north with the last of the regime, and both men die. It is not clean. Almost everyone else dies first.

The butcher's bill. Delfin (Jaime Fabregas), Victor (Raymart Santiago), Roxanne (Shaina Magdayao), James (Jay Gonzaga), Dante (Bassilyo) and Marsial (Smugglaz) are all killed before the operation is done. Jerome (John Prats) and Rigor (Marc Solis) are hogtied by Lucio's gunmen and dressed as attackers, and are shot by their own side. Then Diana (Angel Aquino), Billy (John Medina), Greco (Sancho delas Alas), Ramil (Michael de Mesa) and Patrick (CJ Ramos) stay behind to cover Cardo and President Oscar Hidalgo (Rowell Santiago), knowing what it will cost. Cardo lures the enemy away to buy the president time, takes a bullet that looks fatal, and Oscar finds the angle that lets him kill Renato.

The hospital and the grave. Cardo recovers. His grandmother Flora is dead, and the tombstone in the final scenes carries Susan Roces' real birth and death dates, because the actress died in May 2022 and the show was rewritten around it. Cardo goes to the grave to say goodbye.

And then he goes home. He decides to return to the province and start again, and the series ends with a new beginning rather than a conclusion. As ABS-CBN News put it on the morning after the broadcast, the show "concluded Friday with the title character portrayed by Coco Martin returning to the province and starting anew, marking the end of an era for Philippine television." The farewell drew a record 536,000-plus concurrent YouTube viewers, and the hashtags #FPJsAngProbinsyano and #FPJAP7MissionAccomplished alternately took turns at the top of Twitter's trending list.
