# Ramayana (2026) Review: Release Date, Box Office, and Part 2

Ramayana: Rise of a Legend opened on November 5, 2026 as the biggest production Indian cinema has attempted, and the opening evidence says the gamble mostly landed. Rotten Tomatoes has it at 76 percent from 51 critics with a 93 percent Popcornmeter from more than 500 verified ratings, and Box Office Mojo books $41.44 million worldwide in its first days, all checked November 10, 2026. Nitesh Tiwari splits the Valmiki epic across two films, and Part One carries the setup: Ranbir Kapoor's Rama, Yash's Ravana, Sai Pallavi's Sita. The Metacritic Metascore of 58 marks exactly where the warm and cold halves of the critics' table meet.

> **Spoiler-free verdict:** A two-part epic built on a canvas Indian cinema has never attempted, with the most consistent praise landing on Yash's Ravana and the score from Hans Zimmer and A.R. Rahman. The critics' table splits between amazement and impatience, 76 percent positive on Rotten Tomatoes against a 58 Metascore, while audiences sit at 93 percent. ## What Ramayana is about

The film runs on the story the world knows and does not apologise for running on it. TMDB's synopsis keeps it at altitude: the marriage of a prince and a princess, an exile, and the start of a legendary journey. Ranbir Kapoor plays Rama, though TMDB's credit reads wider than that: Prince Rama, Parshuram and Lord Vishnu in a single billing, which tells you the film is staging the divine ancestry rather than burying it. Sai Pallavi plays Sita, Yash plays Ravana, Ravi Dubey plays Lakshmana, and the elders carry names audiences can recite before the lights go down: Arun Govil as Dashrath, Lara Dutta as Kaikeyi, Kajal Aggarwal as Mandodari, Sheeba Chaddha as Manthara.

Two pieces of casting history do work in the marketing. Arun Govil, the Ram of the 1987 television serial, returns to the story as King Dashrath, and Metacritic's top cast page lists Amitabh Bachchan as the narrator. Anupam Kher voices Jatayu. The shape of the production, produced by Namit Malhotra with Prime Focus Studios co-producing alongside DNEG and Yash's Monster Mind Creations, sits inside the claim that this is the most expensive film project India has attempted. The Economic Times used those exact words in its release-week coverage; the Times of India put it the same way in its live blog.

## A two-part epic and its numbers

The public budget record carries two figures. TMDB carries a production budget of $260 million for Part One. The figure most people repeat, Rs 4,000 crore, is the two-film total, and B.H. Harsh in The New Indian Express framed his 3/5 review around it, calling the film "a visual beast of epic proportion" and "one gamble that entirely rides on its scale, the much-touted 4000 crore budget." Box Office Mojo's early gross, checked November 10, 2026, stands at $8.65 million domestic against $32.79 million international, with India's opening alone at $32 million and a $6.24 million domestic opening on the November 6 wide US date.

This is a first-film investment with a second film behind it, and a $41.44 million worldwide total from TMDB, matching Box Office Mojo to the dollar, is the number at the time of writing. The Metacritic user score says the demand side is not hypothetical: Universal Acclaim at 8.8 from 80 user ratings. The Metascore beneath it, 58 from 9 critic reviews, splits 44 percent positive, 44 percent mixed, 11 percent negative, which is one of the more even four-four-one tables Metacritic has posted this year.

## What the critics say

The positive column leads with wonder at the ambition. Peter Bradshaw at The Guardian filed an 80 and described "the forthright, unselfconscious storytelling energy that popular Hollywood films had in the 1950s and 60s, the era of sword-and-sandal," closing with "It's a rather amazing adventure." Aparita Bhandari at The Globe and Mail rated it 75 and wrote that the film is "a spectacle to behold" with "enough of a story to hold together the CGI set pieces." Brandon Zachary at Screen Rant gave it 70 and called it "a gorgeously realized film, with a solid mix of flashy special effects and impressive stunt work."

The middle of the table holds the interesting verdicts. Nicolas Rapold at The New York Times put it at 60 with the film surging "unmistakably whenever Tiwari can break out of dutiful dialogues to stage grand set pieces." Ian Freer at Empire, also 60, called it "overlong and overwrought" but "broadly entertaining," and credited the set pieces, flying elephants included. At Rotten Tomatoes, William Bibbiani of TheWrap praised how Tiwari keeps "this gigantic, episodic story from ever feeling dull," and Elliott Collins of Movie Files went to 3.5/5 on "a monumental cinematic experience" admitting the time jumps cost the film emotional impact.

The dissent is specific and worth quoting whole. Siddhant Adlakha at IGN filed a 40: "A majestic epic falls victim to clunky action, eye-sore CGI, and awkward AI lip-syncing in what is, unfortunately, just a Part 1." Simon Abrams at RogerEbert.com went 1/4 and wrote that it is hard to imagine anything in the depiction of Rama and Ravana's rivalry "that you can't find elsewhere." Kevin Maher at The Times of London, 2/5 in the RT pull-quote, called the film "tiresome, sluggish and po-faced." Those are the numbers behind the 58.

## Ramayana release date

India and the worldwide opening landed on November 5, 2026, positioned for the Diwali window. Box Office Mojo's territory table puts the earliest release on November 5 across EMEA and APAC regions, with India booking $32 million on day one, Thailand at $550,000, the Netherlands at $240,000, and the US wide release counted from November 6. Hindustan Times reported paid preview shows in the United States and Canada on November 4 ahead of the Friday openers. The Economic Times recorded first shows in India from 6:15 a.m., and the Times of India's live blog reported Rs 49.75 crore in opening-day advance bookings including block seats. The release serves Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada and Malayalam audiences, per the Times of India, and the US carries the PG-13 certificate TMDB lists on its wide entry.

One technical footnote belongs here. The listed runtimes disagree: TMDB carries 201 minutes, Rotten Tomatoes and Box Office Mojo both list 3 hours 15 minutes, and Metacritic's details page says 2 hours 45 minutes. We present the TMDB figure and flag the rest of the table rather than settle one page against another.

## Ramayana Part 2 and the 2028 shift

The two-part plan changed the week the first film opened. Producer Namit Malhotra confirmed Part 2 has moved from a 2027 window to 2028, and Hindustan Times carries his reasoning: "the idea was now that we've got so much excitement that the world has had, we want to take a little bit more time to make sure that when we present the grand finale, we have a little bit more time to do what we did on Part One." He added that the extra year is "what I genuinely believe we need to do justice for a film of this size." NDTV reported the same confirmation, plus the wrinkle that the project has become two master versions: the English dub needed its own edit and a script rewrite "so that it can be presented in its own original form." Malhotra also told E! News the originally designed shape was an Indian film for global audiences, which is exactly the demand the Metacritic user table is registering. A trilogy is possible but unconfirmed, so treat any headline about a third film as a question until the studio answers it.

## The verdict

Ramayana earns its place in the 2026 conversation the way its producer billed it: as a bet that Indian cinema could carry an epic at Hollywood-scale engineering and be judged on the world stage by the same table that judges Hollywood. The bet reads as a win on the evidence available: 76 percent from 51 critics, 93 percent from audiences who rated it in the days after opening, a 58 against a critics' column whose negatives are specific rather than general, and $41.44 million worldwide in under a week with two downstream markets still to open. The complaints that survive every read are about pace, dialogue and the seams where set pieces meet story, and they are the ones Empire and the New York Times float while the Guardian and Screen Rant open the wonder end of the table. A first part with a second film parked a year out carries its own logic; the audience wrote its verdict early. **Rating: 8.0/10 (SAMPLE, owner adjusts).**
