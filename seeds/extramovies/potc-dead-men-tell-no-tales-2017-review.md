Dead Men Tell No Tales is the Pirates of the Caribbean film where the series remade itself and lost. Joachim Rønning and Espen Sandberg's 2017 entry rebuilds the 2003 plot with new parts: a cursed captain out of Jack's past, a crew of the dead, and one treasure that can break every curse at sea. The film runs 129 minutes, cost $230 million, and grossed $795,922,298 worldwide, per TMDB. Rotten Tomatoes logged a 30% Tomatometer from 290 reviews, and Metacritic logged a 39 Metascore from 45 critics, both checked November 12, 2026. Javier Bardem and Geoffrey Rush earn their keep. Everything else gets traded away, and the first thing to go is the compass.

> **Spoiler-free verdict:** A fifth Pirates film that remakes the first one and bets the series' central object on a bottle. Javier Bardem's Salazar is a real villain, and the Devil's Triangle prologue plays with dread the rest of the film cannot hold. Barbossa's final hour is the best character work the series has managed since the original trilogy. The middle drowns in digital murk and the new leads stay furniture. Worth it for Salazar and Rush. Skip it if the compass mythology was ever the point.

## What Dead Men Tell No Tales is about

The Silent Mary, a Spanish warship, chases a Navy squadron into the Devil's Triangle and dies there in fire and water. Captain Armando Salazar survives as something worse. His crew keeps sailing, his face hangs off his skull in strips, and every pirate in reach dies. He spares one boy, Henry Turner, and sends him home with a threat for Jack.

The boy grows into Henry (Brenton Thwaites) and hunts Poseidon's Trident, the one object that can break his father Will's (Orlando Bloom) curse aboard the Flying Dutchman. Jack is in St. Martin with his luck spent. His crew drags the town's bank through the streets behind horses, and the vault arrives with the money gone. The crew deserts him, Jack trades his compass for a drink, and the trade opens the Triangle's gate.

The third party arrives as Carina Smyth (Kaya Scodelario), an astronomer sentenced to hang as a witch because she carries her father's diary, a book whose star map points to the Trident. Jack, Henry, and Carina escape the gallows through a guillotine gag that keeps sending Jack under the blade. Barbossa (Geoffrey Rush) returns as a salvage magnate with a fleet, a deal for Salazar, and a claim on the same trident. The Black Pearl comes back out of a magic bottle at the exact moment the chase needs it.

Per TMDB, the film opened May 23, 2017, and the US theatrical row carries PG-13.

## Cast and performances

Bardem is the reason to watch. Salazar is the first Pirates villain since Davy Jones with a physical idea, a face held together by grievance and black tar. His prologue massacre is the one sequence the film stages wide enough to read.

Rush gets the film's best material and finishes it. Barbossa enters as a rich salvage hunter who knows exactly what the diary is. The end payoff is the only moment in the film that earns its silence.

Scodelario fights for the one three-dimensional new character. Carina is an astronomer in an era that hangs women for science, and Scodelario plays the hanging-row dignity straight. The Hollywood Reporter's John DeFore called her "just about the only member of the cast who seems to believe she's expected to be more than a thin generic functionary or flamboyant scene-stealer."

Thwaites plays Henry as a quest with a pulse, and the film asks him to carry a romance it schedules between chases. Depp runs Jack as the punchline. The Irish Times' Tara Brady named "Depp's panto drunkenness" among the film's failures, and the script agrees with her. Jack enters the film drunk in a bank vault and spends the rest of it escaping punishments framed as gags.

## What works

Salazar's arrival is the film's best stretch. The Silent Mary sails through the wreckage with her hull opened like broken teeth. Her dead crew walks the deck with their hair swimming like smoke, and for two minutes the film stages action wide enough to follow. The ghost-shark attack later is the one other purely visual idea, and it lands inside the murk too.

Barbossa's redemption is the second asset. The diary reveal gives Rush a scene the franchise owed him since 2007, and his death buys back feeling the film spent everywhere else. The Black Pearl's growth out of the bottle is the film's one unambiguous crowd-pleaser, and the sea-split finale gives the series its biggest image since the maelstrom of At World's End.

The franchise memory is the third asset. On Stranger Tides skipped Will Turner, Elizabeth Swann, and the compass debt. This film remembers all three, ties Henry to the Dutchman's curse, and gives the compass a plot consequence with teeth.

## What doesn't

The compass bet is the film's core failure. The first film established the compass as the object Jack guarded above everything, and its needle pointed at whatever its holder wanted most. This film has Jack spend it on a bottle inside the first hour and builds its entire plot on the consequence. The trade plays as a joke. The plot pays the bill straight.

Jack's degradation is the second failure. Depp plays the whole film as a man who has given up, and the script agrees with the performance. Every scene treats his drunkenness as the punchline, and the film hands him back the Pearl at the end without asking the compass question again.

The staging murk is the third failure. Outside the prologue and the finale, the action drowns in night blues and digital spray, and the cuts shave each fight down to fragments. The Irish Times' Brady left the line that fits: "There is $230 million of CG effects that never look like anything more than pixels." Rolling Stone's Peter Travers, at 25 on Metacritic, left four words: "Bloated, boring, repetitive, draining."

The rehash is the structural failure underneath. Swap the Trident for Aztec gold and the plot is the 2003 film's, down to a rescue at the gallows and a villain whose grudge on Jack predates the series' first scene. David Wenham's naval commander exists to menace Carina with a rope and exits when the plot stops needing him.

## What audiences say

The audience held better than the critics, and still far below the opener. The Popcornmeter sits at 60% from 100,000+ ratings. Metacritic users read 6.2 from 960 ratings. TMDB carries 6.7 from 13,218 votes. All checked November 12, 2026. That places the film 26 points below the 2003 opener's 86% on the same meter and 6 points above On Stranger Tides' 54%. The franchise kept the crowd and lost the argument.

## Verdict

Is Pirates of the Caribbean: Dead Men Tell No Tales worth watching? Only for Salazar and Barbossa's last hour. Rønning and Sandberg remade the 2003 film in murkier water. Jack trades the series' central object for a bottle. The film calls the trade a joke, and the plot pays the bill in full. The $795,922,298 gross against $230 million, per TMDB, shows the audience arrived and got a rerun. The 30% Tomatometer and the 39 Metascore are the receipts. The rating sits under On Stranger Tides because a sequel that trades away its own mythology for a bar gag loses more than a sequel that arrived with nothing at all. Salazar, Rush, and the Pearl's return are real. The compass bet costs more.
