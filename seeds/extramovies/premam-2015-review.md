# Premam Ending Explained and Full Movie Review

The Premam ending explained in one line: Malar remembers George and says nothing about it. ## What Premam is about

Premam follows George David through three stages, and each stage ends the same way, with George losing the woman he wanted. He is sixteen in Aluva, hovering around Mary with his friends Shambu and Koya, convinced that timing is the only thing standing between them. Timing is not the only thing. Mary already has a boyfriend, also named George, and the teenager who cannot get near her ends up helping her meet him. The chapter closes on a failed set of exams and a decision to grow up.

In 2005 he lands at Union Christian College in Aluva with the same two friends, and the object of the film becomes Malar, a new lecturer from Tamil Nadu. She is the first woman who appears to choose him back. She helps his college dance team, she laughs at his jokes, and for a stretch the film plays like a courtship that is actually working. Faculty members circle her too: Vimal Sir, known as Java, and the physical education instructor Shivan. George does not read them as competition, and the film does not either.

Then Malar goes to Kodaikanal with her cousin Arivazhagan, the groom her family has lined up. The college gets word of a serious accident and her resignation. George and his friends drive up to see her, and she does not recognise him. The trauma has taken selective amnesia with it, along with everything the previous chapter built. George steps back instead of waiting, she marries Arivazhagan, and the three friends attend the wedding reception in silence to say a goodbye nobody inside the room hears.

The third chapter opens in 2014. George is thirty, running a successful café with his childhood friend Jojo, and he meets Celine, twenty-two, who turns out to be the same girl who used to walk to school beside Mary. She is engaged to Roney Varghese, a volatile man who mistreats her, and when the engagement collapses he keeps harassing her until George, Shambu, Koya and Jojo corner him and beat him. George and Celine marry. Premam ends at that reception.

## Why the three-chapter structure holds

Puthren wrote, directed and edited the film himself, and the editing is the structural argument. Each chapter jumps forward in years without announcing it, the supporting faces stay roughly constant, and George's posture changes more than his face does. The friends Shambu and Koya are the continuity device: they age with him, they carry the same jokes across decades, and they are the only people who appear in all three losses.

The casting does similar work. The production introduced seventeen newcomers to Malayalam cinema, and the film leans on that freshness instead of on star weight. Anupama Parameswaran plays Mary as a girl who is unaware of the damage she causes, Madonna Sebastian plays Celine as someone who has watched George longer than he has watched her, and Sai Pallavi, in her film debut, gets the heaviest lift, because Malar has to be believable both as a woman falling for a student and as a woman with an entire life outside his point of view.

The craft is specific rather than glossy. Anend C. Chandran shoots Aluva like a place with weather, the college corridors do most of the flirting, and Rajesh Murugesan's score and songs, including "Malare" and the Anirudh Ravichander sung "Rockaankuthu", carry the emotional cues the dialogue refuses to spell out. At 157 minutes on TMDB, the film is long, and it earns the length by changing registers instead of repeating scenes.

## Premam ending explained

![Premam ending explained: Nivin Pauly and Sai Pallavi in the Premam poster](https://image.tmdb.org/t/p/w500/wfMgsfDrtouYOM6MbrkHtU96Xij.jpg)

The final scene takes place at George and Celine's wedding reception, and Celine has personally invited Malar. Malar arrives with Arivazhagan. As the two of them leave, their conversation carries the whole ending: Malar tells her husband that she is happy for George and that he should be left alone, and the exchange makes clear that her memory of their relationship has come back. She chooses not to tell George. They look at each other once, with recognition on both sides, and the film stops there.

That is the answer to the question the film raises, and it is also where the second question starts. Premam never states when Malar's memory returned, or whether the amnesia was ever as total as the hospital scenes implied. The New Indian Express, writing on the film's fifth anniversary, called the ending open ended and left it to the viewer's judgement, and that reading is the honest one: the film gives you the recovered memory as a fact in the final minute and refuses you the timeline. Hindustan Times, marking ten years, put the sharper version of the same complaint, arguing that Malar exists only as far as George can see her and that the film never tells us what she wants outside that frame.

The criticism and the ending are therefore the same object. Premam has spent two and a half hours training George to accept loss, and the last beat gives the acceptance back to Malar, who has to perform contentment at a party for a man who once drove to Kodaikanal for her. Read as criticism rather than as stated intent, the final glance is not closure for George. It is the film admitting that he got the cleaner ending, and that she carried the cost of it off screen.

## What the critics said

Times of India rated Premam 3.5 out of 5, noting that the film never attempts anything profound and praising Nivin Pauly for unearthing beauty in trifles. The single critic review logged on Rotten Tomatoes, from Mythily Ramachandran in February 2020, highlights Puthren's unconventional narration and the freshness of the humour. The Indian Express, on the tenth anniversary in May 2025, revisited the marketing line that Premam was the second film in the history of world cinema with nothing fresh, and asked how a movie sold that way ended up feeling fresh. Its reputation has held up in the places that keep lists: The Hindu included it among its top 25 Malayalam films of the decade.

## The verdict

Premam works because it treats rejection as the plot rather than as an obstacle, and because it lets the last scene belong to Malar instead of to the couple. The TMDB rating of 7.6 from 133 votes and the IMDb rating of 8.3 from about 29,000 ratings both point the same way, and the 77 percent Tomatometer, thin as one review is, does not disagree. It is a long film with a tight structure, and the ending repays the patience.
