On Stranger Tides is the Pirates of the Caribbean film where the machinery won and the movie lost. Rob Marshall's 2011 entry cut the ensemble in half, cut the runtime to 136 minutes, was produced for $379 million, and grossed $1,046,721,266 worldwide, per TMDB. The critics' answer was a 32% Tomatometer from 270 reviews and a Metascore of 45 from 39 critics, with a consensus blaming "a disjointed plot and a non-stop barrage of noisy action sequences," all checked November 10, 2026. Penélope Cruz and one mermaid attack earn the film real credit. The quest around them never earns its own existence.

> **Spoiler-free verdict:** A leaner, louder Pirates entry with nothing at stake in it. Cruz gives the film its only live-wire performance, the mermaid hunt is one properly staged set piece, and the 136-minute runtime is the shortest in the series. The story is a three-way hike to a spring, the action cuts until the geography dissolves, and the crew table lost the chemistry that made the first three films run. Worth it for Cruz, Rush, and the mermaids. Skip it if you need a plot that pays.

## What On Stranger Tides is about

London, and a Jack Sparrow impersonator is recruiting a crew. The impersonator turns out to be Angelica (Penélope Cruz), a woman Jack left at a convent years ago, and she recruits for Blackbeard (Ian McShane), who needs the Fountain of Youth before a prophecy catches up with him. Jack wakes aboard the Queen Anne's Revenge with a zombie quartermaster over him and learns his escape plans mean nothing against Blackbeard's voodoo. A cloth doll stitched to look like Jack gets squeezed, and Jack's body obeys.

Three parties race for the fountain. Blackbeard has the ship and Angelica, who turns out to be his daughter. Barbossa (Geoffrey Rush) lost a leg to Blackbeard, now sails for the Crown as a privateer, and wants the pirate dead more than he wants the fountain. The Spanish fleet wants the whole business shut down. The quest needs two silver chalices from Ponce de León's wreck and one mermaid's tear, and the crew gets the tear by netting a mermaid in the shallows and leaving her to die in a fish tank. A missionary, Philip (Sam Claflin), and the captured mermaid Syrena (Àstrid Bergès-Frisbey) carry the film's only sincere subplot.

Per TMDB, the film runs 136 minutes, opened May 15, 2011, was produced for $379 million, and grossed $1,046,721,266 worldwide.

## Cast and performances

Cruz is the reason to watch. Angelica has a con artist's training, a daughter's grudge, and the film's only argument that changes anyone's mind. Cruz plays her blade-sharp against Depp, and the scenes between them carry the friction the script keeps promising everywhere else. She is also the only person on screen who seems to want something the plot cannot hand her.

Rush rebuilds Barbossa with a wooden leg, a Crown commission, and a grudge he feeds on schedule. His final accounting with Blackbeard is the one plot thread the film completes properly. McShane glowers through Blackbeard as a man already dead and furious about it, and the part is thinner than his performance. Depp runs the Sparrow routine on autopilot. David Denby wrote in The New Yorker that Depp "seems irrelevant and bored most of the time," and the film's own footage backs him up. Claflin and Bergès-Frisbey do sincere work, but the missionary-and-mermaid thread stays flat from first look to last.

## What works

The mermaid hunt is the film. Marshall sends Blackbeard's crew into waist-deep water at night with nets and lanterns, and for two minutes the picture has dread, rhythm, and readable geography in one place. Bergès-Frisbey's Syrena arrives as a real presence in those scenes, and the sequence would survive on its own.

The runtime is the second asset. At 136 minutes the film is shorter than every Verbinski entry, and the front half moves from London to ship to quest with little dead water. The shorter frame also gives Barbossa's revenge a proper landing, and Rush's payoff is the only beat where the film's two halves, the plot and the characters, meet.

## What doesn't

The staging collapses the action. Marshall cuts the fights into close-ups and coverage fragments until a chase is a collection of arms and spray, and the reader loses the ships. Rotten Tomatoes' critics consensus calls it "a non-stop barrage of noisy action sequences," and David Edelstein's line in New York Magazine went further: "I've never seen a film in which what was actually onscreen seemed so irrelevant." The distance between spending and showing is the film's defining flaw.

The cast hole is the second deduction. Bloom and Knightley are gone, and the replacement leads bring nothing to the table. Philip and Syrena act in a film that treats them as a plot device with feelings. The quest itself is the third: three parties walk to the same spring, the route throws one obstacle at a time, and no antagonist on the road threatens to change the ending until the fountain is already in reach. Blackbeard is a walking deadline, so the film has urgency without danger.

## What audiences say

The audience numbers held far better than the critics' numbers. The Popcornmeter sits at 54% from 100,000+ ratings, TMDB carries 6.6 from 15,421 votes, and Metacritic users read 6.3 from 921 ratings, all checked November 10, 2026. That is a real drop from the 86% audience score the 2003 opener holds on the same site. The franchise kept its viewers that year and lost their enthusiasm.

## Verdict

Is Pirates of the Caribbean: On Stranger Tides worth watching? Only for Cruz, Rush, and one mermaid attack. Marshall made a shorter film and forgot to make it a tighter one, the action staging went unreadable at the exact moment the franchise needed its new director to prove the handover worked, and the billion-dollar gross, $1,046,721,266 against $379 million per TMDB, shows the audience arrived and found nothing to argue about afterward. The 32% Tomatometer and the 45 Metascore are the receipts. The deductions are structural, and the fifth film inherited all of them.
