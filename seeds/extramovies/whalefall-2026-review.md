Yes, Whalefall is worth watching, and it is worth watching in a cinema with the sound turned up. Brian Duffield drops Austin Abrams into the stomach of a sperm whale and holds him there for 106 minutes, and for long stretches the film is so convincingly built that the whale stops being a trick. Then the flashbacks start pulling, and a survival thriller turns into a son having it out with his dead father. Rotten Tomatoes says 72 percent from 168 critic reviews and Metacritic says 59 from 37, checked October 21, 2026. Both numbers are right.

## What Whalefall is about

Brian Duffield's Whalefall is a survival thriller with one location, one problem and one hour. Jay Gardiner (Austin Abrams) is a Californian who decamped to the Midwest to get away from his father. Mitt Gardiner (Josh Brolin) was the local dive-boat captain, and Entertainment Weekly opens the film on the end of his life: mesothelioma, sealed letters for his wife and three children left in a cooler on the boat, then diving weights and a jump into the ocean. The body is never found, and the family buries an empty casket.

A year later Jay dives alone to find it, and, as Entertainment Weekly has it, without telling his mother Zara (Elisabeth Shue) or his sisters Eva (Jane Levy) and Nan (Emily Rudd), and without authorization. Off the edge of Monterey Bay, a giant squid is fighting a sperm whale. The squid's tentacles catch Jay's gear and the whale swallows both of them. Jay wakes up in one of the whale's stomach chambers with about an hour of air left in his tank, and the film is about what he does with it.

The film adapts Daniel Kraus's 2023 novel, and Kraus co-wrote the screenplay with Duffield.

## Cast and performances

Abrams does the heavy lifting, and he does it mostly without words. His regulator is clamped in his mouth because the air inside the whale is toxic, so the film takes his voice away and leaves him wheezing, bleeding and staring. IGN's Jim Vejvoda called him "an exceptional Austin Abrams anchors the whole endeavor with grueling dedication." Resident Evil opened four weeks earlier, in September, and in both films he refuses to play a victim.

Brolin plays Mitt as a hard man with a hard mouth. The Daily Beast's Nick Schager thought the performance overshoots, and put it plainly: Brolin is asked to play "such a grade-A jerk that he invariably chews the scenery." Schager's objection is the film's objection. Mitt is written as a father who is wrong in the way fathers are sometimes wrong, and the movie needs him to be exactly that and nothing more.

Elisabeth Shue plays Zara, Jay's mother, and gets the least of the movie. Jane Levy and Emily Rudd play the sisters, and the film gives them almost nothing to do.

## What works

The whale. Empire's Simon Crook called the opening, a giant squid fighting a sperm whale with Jay caught in the middle, "an awesome, ominous, goosebump-plucking recreation." Inside, the set is lit "in sickly phosphorescent green," the heart working like "the artillery drum of a colossal heart counting down like a booming clock." Nothing there reads as a gimmick. It is a room you can smell.

The production built the thing rather than implying it. In a September 7, 2026 Deadline piece on the California shoot, Dessi Gomez reported that the stomach models came from the Weta Workshop in New Zealand, while the head, throat, esophagus and larynx had to be built in California because they were too big to ship in time. The whale head is 12 feet tall and 18 feet long.

The camera and the sound. Aaron Morton shot it, and RogerEbert.com's Zachary Lee noticed how he frames Jay "as though his surroundings are assaulting him." On the sound, Lee was brief: "Sequences like these are further amplified by the stellar sound department, which makes it all feel otherworldly." The whale's interior is a place you hear before you understand.

The scale of the idea. No other film this year puts a man inside a whale and stays there. That is the achievement, and the reason the film is worth the ticket even when the flashbacks sag.

## What doesn't

The flashbacks. Rotten Tomatoes' own consensus catches the fault line: "Unforgettably visceral whenever it isn't getting mired in flashbacks." Every time the film cuts away to Jay as a boy on a boat with his father, the tension leaks out, because the film trades a man with an hour of air for a man being told off.

The third act. Benjamin Lee in The Guardian was blunter: "it just doesn't work as a 106-minute adventure, too overstretched and too silly to have us rooting for his escape." He thought the picture could have been a lean 85-minute race to survive instead, and The Daily Beast's Nick Schager found the same seam, where the film stops being a survival picture and starts being a father-and-son drama that keeps asking you to cry on schedule. Richard Whittaker in The Austin Chronicle put the diagnosis in one line: "Whalefall is a tonal mess". His complaint is the film's own, a movie that puts gross-out comedy next to a family tearjerker and expects you to enjoy both.

The framing. The film insists on a single location and then keeps leaving it. A movie called Whalefall spends a lot of its running time in a whale and a lot of the rest in a California kitchen and a dive boat. The thrills need air, and the flashbacks take it.

The business. The film cost $60 million and opened to roughly $8 million in the United States on 3,065 screens, finishing fourth in a weekend led by Street Fighter and an Elizabeth Holmes documentary, with the box office numbers as they stood on October 19, 2026. Deadline's October 17, 2026 report put the CinemaScore at B and pointed at the competition: Street Fighter is "the bigger IP with a similar exit," and its pull on older men was "walloping Whalefall's business, the overlap in male demos."

## What audiences say

Rotten Tomatoes has the film at 72 percent from 168 critic reviews and the top critics at 64 percent from 33, checked October 21, 2026. The Popcornmeter sits at 81 percent from more than 500 verified ratings, or 76 percent from more than 1,000 ratings when all of them count, checked the same day.

Metacritic has it at 59 from 37 critic reviews, checked October 21, 2026, which the site labels "Mixed or Average," split 54 percent positive, 41 percent mixed and 5 percent negative. The user score there is 6.7 from 28 ratings. Letterboxd's member average is 3.25 out of 5 from 92,965 ratings and 52,550 written reviews, checked the same day. TMDB has it at 6.6 from 67 votes and JustWatch scores it 6.8 from about 4,100 ratings, both checked the same day.

The split is the story. On Metacritic the scores run from a 90 at Collider to a 30 at The Austin Chronicle, and the people who loved the film and the people who did not were watching the same 106 minutes. Whittaker reached the opposite conclusion, calling it a tonal mess, and there is a fair case for that reading.

## Verdict

Is Whalefall worth watching? Yes, and see it in a cinema, because the sound design carries the first half and the third act needs a room that can take it. Brian Duffield made the strangest survival film of 2026 and then asked his editor to protect the family scenes. The whale is the movie. The father is the theme the movie keeps interrupting itself to make.

The numbers say the same thing from four directions: Rotten Tomatoes says 72 percent and Metacritic says 59, both checked October 21, 2026, Letterboxd says 3.25 out of 5 on the same day, and CinemaScore says B. That is a good film with a bad third act, and an exceptional lead performance inside a place no one else is filming this year.

Watch it with the volume up. The last thirty minutes are where the movie decides what it thinks it is.

## Spoiler warning: the ending of Whalefall

The whale dies before Jay escapes it. Orcas attack the animal, a nearby pod of sperm whales drives the predators off, and the wounded whale eventually succumbs to its injuries. Jay hears his father in the whale's body, and the voice tells him what he has to do. Screen Rant's Josh Bate describes the rest: Jay "puts together oil found in the whale's stomach, combined with batteries he brought and his oxygen tank, to create a blast that blows a giant hole in the animal's stomach." Entertainment Weekly's Patrick Gomez has him rigging "an explosive out of his oxygen tank and other materials he finds" to blow a hole through the stomach wall.

He lives. EW says he is "pulled from the water by a passing boat," alive but with busted eardrums, many broken bones and severe acid scarring on his face and hands. The film jumps forward an undisclosed amount of time, to Jay on a boat with his mother and sisters, "now without a left hand." He is finally ready to read the letter his father left him before taking his life. He smiles as he reads, and the film never tells the audience what it says.

Richard Whittaker in The Austin Chronicle gave Jay four ways out: get vomited out through the mouth, climb up the blowhole, take the aft exit, or make his own egress. He comes up short on the first three and lands on the fourth. Benjamin Lee in The Guardian did not buy the final scene, calling the last conversation between Jay and his dead father "laughably ineffective" and noting that the questions Mitt finally gets to ask include "did you ever want a dog?" Empire's Simon Crook took the other side of that same ending, finding "a surprisingly poignant closing shot."

TIME's Megan McCluskey headed her review "Whalefall Is the Most Disgusting Tearjerker You'll See This Year" and wrote that the ending is "overly sappy and obviously a bit preposterous." TIME also quoted Josh Brolin, talking to The Direct about what the whale stands for, calling it a symbol of "how to get out of our own way in order to inhale those gems that you need." The line the film leaves Jay with at the end is "The whale is forever."
