Dead Man's Chest is the Pirates sequel where one performance raised the ceiling for computer-generated characters and the story never climbs to meet it. Gore Verbinski's 2006 entry holds a 53% Tomatometer from 227 reviews and a 53 Metascore from 37 critics, checked live on November 12, 2026, and it grossed $1,066,179,747 worldwide on a $200,000,000 production budget, per TMDB. Bill Nighy's Davy Jones is the best thing this franchise ever built. The film around him is half a story that stops on a cliff instead of ending.

> **Spoiler-free verdict:** Worth it for Davy Jones, the water-wheel swordfight, and the grim Flying Dutchman scenes. The middle sags into a cannibal-island comedy detour, the plot is all setup with no payoff, and the ending is a hard cut toward a third film. Verbinski's craft holds every set piece together. The story cannot. Treat it as the first half of one very long movie.

## What Dead Man's Chest is about

Three parties want the same box. Lord Cutler Beckett (Tom Hollander) arrests Will Turner and Elizabeth Swann on their wedding day and offers them the gallows or a job: bring him Jack Sparrow's compass. Jack owes Davy Jones his soul: Jones raised the Black Pearl from the seabed thirteen years ago, and the debt has come due. Will's search for Jack lands him aboard the Dutchman instead, where his supposedly dead father Bootstrap Bill (Stellan Skarsgård) works the rigging, his skin fused into the hull. Elizabeth breaks out of prison on her own and turns up at a voodoo table run by Tia Dalma (Naomie Harris).

The compass points at whatever its holder wants most, and the plot is a chain of thefts built around that. Jack wants the compass so it can lead him to the Dead Man's Chest, which holds the beating heart of Davy Jones; whoever holds the heart commands the Dutchman. Beckett wants the compass for the same prize. Norrington (Jack Davenport), disgraced since the first film, wants Jack's head as his route back to a commission. The chest is dug up on Isla Cruces, and the fight for it produces the mill sequence: Will, Jack, and Norrington dueling on top of a rolling water wheel while the wheel carries them out of the mill, down the hill, and into the surf.

The film ends with the kraken dragging the Black Pearl under, Jack chained to the mast, and the last scene finds Tia Dalma offering the crew a way to sail after him. The man who can navigate to World's End walks down the stairs, and it is Barbossa (Geoffrey Rush), restored to life with an apple in his hand.

Per TMDB, the film runs 151 minutes, carries a July 6, 2006 release date, was produced for $200,000,000, and grossed $1,066,179,747 worldwide.

## Cast and performances

Nighy built Davy Jones out of a tentacled beard and a crab claw, and the performance-capture work still holds up two decades later. He keeps the key to his own chest on a cord at his neck, and the organ solos he plays with his beard give the villain an interior life the plot never mines. Metacritic's awards record for the film lists one Academy Award win and four nominations, and the effects work is the reason.

Depp runs Jack as a man dodging every conversation that could settle his debt, and the dread under the comedy is the film's engine. Tom Hollander plays Beckett as a clerk who owns the world's paperwork and enjoys using it; his scenes carry the quiet menace of a man who never raises his voice because he never needs to. Keira Knightley and Orlando Bloom get the thinnest thread of the three leads. The interrupted wedding starts it, the film keeps Elizabeth and Will moving as pawns between Jack's scheme and Beckett's, and the kraken finale hands Elizabeth the picture's cruelest call: the kiss that leaves Jack chained to the mast.

## What works

The Dutchman is the film. Verbinski shoots Jones' crew as a single decaying organism: sailors merged into wood, a shark-faced crewman, barnacles where faces used to be. The Dutchman rising out of open water still plays as horror inside a blockbuster. The water-wheel duel is the second asset. One rotating set, three fighters, readable geography through every spin; it is the franchise's best pure action scene and a set piece where you always know where everyone stands. Hans Zimmer writes Jones an organ theme that lets the villain score his own scenes, and the kraken attacks give the score its darkest passages.

The gamble structure is the third asset. The film ends with its hero dead and its villain holding every card, and Wikipedia's box-office record shows the risk paid: a $136 million opening weekend in the United States that was a record at the time, the fastest film ever to $1 billion worldwide at 63 days, and the highest-grossing release of 2006.

## What doesn't

The cannibal island episode stops the film. Jack spends it as a crowned chief of a bone-jeweled tribe, the escape runs on a rolling cage gag, and the sequence halts the Jones plot for a long comedic detour that adds nothing but a meme. Rotten Tomatoes' critics consensus names the deeper issue: "Gone is Depp's unpredictability and much of the humor and originality of the first movie." David Ansen wrote in Newsweek that it is "an overproduced movie that tries so strenuously hard to be 'fun' that it's a chore to sit through," and Todd McCarthy's Variety review found "a sense of bloat and where-do-we-go-from-here aimlessness to this unconscionably protracted undertaking."

The plot is the structural flaw. Every scene moves a token; no scene changes what anyone wants. The chest is a MacGuffin with a heartbeat, and the film spends 151 minutes getting it into the right hands so the third film can begin. Maitland McDonagh's TV Guide review called it "an all-you-can-eat buffet" where "everything's tasty, the surfeit is sickening," which is the honest version of the compliment. And the ending is deliberate: it settles nothing and hands the business to the third film.

## Verdict

Is Dead Man's Chest worth watching? Yes, on the film's own terms: it is the first half of a two-part story, told with the franchise's best villain and its best action scene. Verbinski shows every dollar of the $200,000,000 budget on screen, and the story moves tokens toward a third film for two and a half hours. The 53% Tomatometer and the 53 Metascore measure the plot's stall. The 72% Popcornmeter and the $1,066,179,747 gross, per TMDB, measure what audiences found in it anyway. Watch it for Nighy, the wheel, and the kraken. Watch [At World's End](/reviews/potc-at-worlds-end-2007-review) immediately after, because the film demands it.
